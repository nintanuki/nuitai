Why Nuitai exists

A vast, bright, colorful water world of scattered islands with colossal wonders on the horizon, inspired by the nature, cultures and mythology of the Pacific.

That is the world in the author's words. It is being built for a turn-based RPG, and everything on this site is a step toward it, a work in progress.

It started with Final Fantasy X, about twenty years ago, and with being tired of fantasy that is always dark, cold and medieval European. Nuitai is the opposite on purpose: bright water, warm islands, and cultures that fantasy rarely visits, Polynesia and maritime Southeast Asia above all.

From One Piece came the idea that every island should have its own premise, so sailing to a new one feels like opening a new book. And a small country like Kiribati, a few hundred square miles of land scattered over a huge sea, is the feeling of the map: a little land in a lot of ocean.

The circle

Before he knew it had a name, the author drew a circle on a map of the real world: Hawaii, down to New Zealand, across to maritime Southeast Asia and up to Japan. That circle turned out to be almost exactly the Pacific Ring of Fire, the ring of volcanoes around the ocean. "That is my circle!"

So the ring became the rule. Every inspiration comes from inside it, the ring and everything it encloses. Anything from outside has to arrive by sea through a place on the ring, the way India's faiths reached Bali. That is how the Bandari carry a Hindu and Buddhist inheritance into the islands.

The rule cuts things too. Haitian Vodou was part of the dark Matua look for a long time, and it is out, because Haiti is outside the ring. It was always the look that mattered, not the religion, and the ring has its own macabre ancestor traditions to draw on instead: the Toraja of Sulawesi and the carvers of the Sepik.

Laid out as a ring around a whirlpool, Nuitai is about the size of the whole Pacific. The directions mostly match the real ocean; the distances to the west and south are stretched to close the ring.

Small choices with a story

Light is La, not Ra
The Māori word for the sun is rā. Written Ra, it read too much like the Egyptian sun god, so the element is La, and Ra is only how the people of Matua say it.
Ata used to be Aku
The god of darkness and fear was renamed partly to get away from Aku, the villain of Samurai Jack. Ata means shadow in Samoan and Māori.
Kulāiwi, the starting island
Hawaiian for homeland. Taken apart, the word means something like "plain of bones," and it was chosen for the whole word, not its pieces.
Magic is kinesthetic
Every spell needs a motion or a breath. A tied-up mage cannot cast. The word was chosen over the advice that it sounds like a school learning style, because it says exactly the thing.
No drawn map is canon
The real map is a table: every region's direction, distance in miles and width. A program draws the pictures from that table, so a picture can never disagree with the world. The island shapes on the maps are invented.
"Fluxx rules"
After the card game: the stronger a move, the more of a wildcard it should be. Paku's Tidal Wave hits every enemy, then washes his own party around, Paku included.
No MP
"I was never a fan of MP." Moves have waits and cooldowns instead of a magic bar.
People are not Pokémon
Monsters have elemental weaknesses. People do not.
Paku is not Maui
Paku's look comes from Garp in One Piece first, then Whitebeard, Might Guy and All Might. Disney's Maui is the look to stay away from.
Eight hundred years
The Bandari arrived eight centuries ago, around the time the real Polynesian long voyages between Hawaii and Tahiti were about to end for good. Far enough back that nobody alive can settle an argument about the homeland they came from.
Shaka's face is never shown
Art shows his symbols, the sun and his sword in the sand, or his back. It borrows from early Buddhist art, which showed the Buddha only through symbols. And Shaka died choking on breadfruit, a plain death at the end of a sage's life, the way the Buddha died after a meal of spoiled food.

The secrets the wiki keeps

The Luminary of Kota Emas. The old Luminary is dead, and the temple is hiding it. The new Luminary, Tahji, is a child of about eight and a gaja, one of the elephant people, chosen by a test the way a reincarnation is found. The priest who repeats his words is the one really ruling. The curtain and the story of an illness go further than the tradition of the veiled face: they hide how young he is.

The model is the Fifth Dalai Lama, whose death in 1682 his regent hid for about fifteen years.

Still not settled: what it costs when the secret comes out, and whether the old Luminary was a gaja too.

The languages12
Nuitai'lo

Hawaiian, Samoan, Māori. Nuitai'lo's roots are Polynesian, and each accent is modeled on one real language: Hawaiian in the east, Samoan in Velafi, Māori in Matua. Real cognates are the test of a word's history.

Why this way round: Māla Iki is the Hawaii of this world, and the author finds the l softer and gentler. The south keeping the old sounds matches the Callers keeping the oldest form of the faith. This replaced an earlier rule (east Māori, south Hawaiian), which had grown out of two place names: Māra is Māori for garden and Mākua is Hawaiian for parent.

-gata comes from tangata, a person; the real Hawaiian word is kanaka, which is why the east says -naka. Maita holds up because Hawaiian maika'i is Tahitian maita'i. In Matua, the real Māori forms of ahi and pōhatu use h, so those keep it. Māla Iki is Hawaiian: māla, garden, and iki, small. Māla is also Māori māra. Laniakea, the cosmos, is named for the Laniakea Supercluster, Hawaiian for "open skies". Lá'gata, Shaka's title, is after the Buddha's, Tathāgata.

Several names are real words in all three accents. N'garo: Hawaiian nalo is hidden or vanished, Samoan galo is forgotten, Māori ngaro is hidden or lost. Malama is Hawaiian for the lunar month and for light. Tokerau is tied to the north and the north wind in Māori, Samoan to'elau is the trade wind, and Ko'olau is the Hawaiian windward side. Vela, wela and wera are the Samoan, Hawaiian and Māori words for hot. Ata is Hawaiian aka, Samoan and Māori ata, shadow.

The light element was once labeled Ra; Ra was too close to the Egyptian sun god.

Names kept away from: Moana means ocean and the Disney film took it; Moanatu and Moanaroa are near enough to be deliberate and far enough to be safe. Moananuiākea is the real Hawaiian name for the Pacific, so it is not used. Māra, the Matua form of Māla, collides with the demon who tempts the Buddha and with the Elder Scrolls goddess of love; the author finds that interesting rather than a problem.

Of the unassigned roots, lā'au and lapa'au together make lā'au lapa'au, the real Hawaiian term for herbal medicine.

Bandhasa

Malay and Indonesian, with Sanskrit. Bandhasa's words are Malay and Indonesian, with Sanskrit for old and sacred things. Each dialect has a real model: North Matahari is Javanese, Kota Emas is Malacca Malay, South Matahari is Ambonese and Papuan Malay, and the Bayang Seas are Tausug and Sama-Bajau. A dialect changes only how a word is said, never how it is spelled, the way Javanese writes Surabaya and says Suroboyo.

The Bandhasa script

It is to look Southeast Asian. Real scripts to look at: Javanese, Balinese, Baybayin and the old Kawi script. As a cipher it works the way Aurebesh in Star Wars and Al Bhed in Final Fantasy X do.

Malalo

Japanese. Malalo has the sounds of Japanese.

Kamego

Okinawan. Kamego's real model is Okinawan, as Malalo's is Japanese. The Kame say Japanese-shaped words with Okinawan's three short vowels.

The Umigame language

The sounds real turtles make. Turtles were long thought nearly silent. Recordings of dozens of species in 2022 found all of them making sounds: hatchlings click and chirp inside the egg so the nest hatches together, Amazon river turtle mothers answer them with low calls, giant tortoises groan and bellow when courting, red-footed tortoises cluck like chickens, and any turtle hisses as it pulls into its shell.

Pekapeka'ro

The New Zealand short-tailed bat's songs. The New Zealand short-tailed bat. It finds its way by echolocation, calls pitched above human hearing. In the breeding season the males sing from a chosen perch in the roost tree to call the females, and the females come to the best singers.

Mokomoko'lo

The frilled-neck lizard's frill and head bobs. The frilled-neck lizard of Australia and New Guinea. It is nearly silent apart from a hiss and talks with its body: when threatened it flares the frill, gapes its mouth and stands tall, and it bobs its head at rivals.

Geram

The king cobra's growl, and snakes hearing through the ground. The king cobra, the one snake that growls: its hiss is pitched low, close to a dog's. Like every snake it is nearly deaf to sound in the air and feels vibrations through the ground with its jaw and body.

Samego

Japanese, distorted: a harsh cousin of Malalo. Malalo is modeled on Japanese, so this is Japanese bent out of shape: a monster speaking distorted Japanese. Same is Japanese for shark.

The otter people's speech

The otter people's language is drawn on Unangam Tunuu, the Aleut language, and the Ikagata say its words by Japanese sound rules.

Stone Speech

Native South American, for the look. Stone Speech is to look native South American. As a cipher it works the way Aurebesh in Star Wars and Al Bhed in Final Fantasy X do.

Not canon yet

Written, liked, and waiting for a decision.

  • The sky people never left. The ruins show coiling, feathered, wingless serpents on every wall, and everyone took them for decoration. In this draft the Lanigata are those serpents, and the long shapes that turn slowly above the clouds, the ones sailors point out to their children, are them. Still alive, and nobody knows.
  • Pua'a, the god who bled. A boar god of animals and mortals, the only god known to bleed, and already dead. Every island tells his death differently: drunk wrestling, an eating contest. Everyone who hears it says, "Yes, that sounds like him."
  • The boar people in the chants. The oldest migration chants list tusked, red-haired ancestors who stayed behind on a shore no chart records.

Loose threads

  • The sun is crowded. Six gods and beasts have some claim on the sun or its light. That is kept on purpose: it is what splits the faith of Māla in two. The one rule is not to add a seventh.
  • Pirates from a lost homeland. One article says the pirates raided the coasts of Bandar, but nobody has reached Bandar in eight hundred years. The likely fix: the pirates came over with the fleets, and today's crews are their heirs.

The numbers

The battle system was tested by having the computer play thousands of fights. Two things it found: a healer is the most valuable member of a party, and three damage dealers with no support get wiped out. Being able to swap in the bench raised the win rate from 88 out of 100 to 99.
