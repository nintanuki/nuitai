Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark Welcome back. Last time I ended in Kulāiwi Village with a fresh save at the totem, 280 pule in my pocket and Hina sitting at 32 of 160 HP, which I should fix before I walk into anything with tusks.

Mark The plan for this session is the stew. Pork is in, but the shoyu is tied up with Miki and her white-haired brother, and I still do not know the rest of Noelani's list. Above all of that sits the Great Boar at the top of the woods, the hunt I took at the Hunters' Lodge for 1000 pule.

Mark What I want to find out: whether the Lanu posts change anything, who Miki's brother is, and whether the wake tonight is where this opening chapter closes.

Mark First job, Hina's health. I open the menu looking for something to eat. Roast Pork says "Recovers 50 HP," which is exactly what she needs, so I hand it to her.

Mark "I don't eat anything with a face!" Fair enough. That is a nice bit of character delivered through a menu, and it tells me the game is tracking who eats what. Breadfruit only gives 10, so I will not burn it.

Mark While I was in there I found something I missed last time: a Battle Bags tab. Each fighter has a "Grass Sash" with four slots, and Kailo's held three Noni Pulu. So that is why the Seer's Shell never showed up in my fight menu. Items only come into a fight if you pack them. I gave the Shell to Kailo. The idea is good, a little like Final Fantasy XII's limited carry, but nothing told me about it in Episode 1, and that cost me a scan.

Mark Hina's Element page lists "Heal," "Heals 18 HP," but it is a loadout screen and I cannot cast it from the field. I will try the totem; praying there may restore us.

Mark Melia is hiding behind a house. "You found me!" Kailo sends her back to her chores. She is still the kid who wants to be in on everything, and I still think that sling matters later.

Mark The totem is on the Grocer's screen. "The party kneels at the totem and tells the Akua all that has happened." I like that wording; saving is a prayer, which suits a game where the gods are on the map.

Mark My save is titled "The Pork Strikes Back." Chapter names on the save file are a nice touch. The totem did not heal, though. Hina is still on 32. In a Dragon Quest game I would go and find an inn; here I do not know where rest is, so I will lean on her Heal in fights and keep the Noni Pulu in Kailo's sash for emergencies.

Mark North of the Grocer the sign reads "Kulāiwi Forest," but this screen is new to me: a hedge maze of palms with two boars in the middle and two plant things on either side of the entrance. I walk up and the boars jump me. "Preemptive strike!" and there are three of them.

Mark New on the command menu: "Cook." I will look at it when I have a moment.

Mark I am building mana with Light strokes and learning something I half saw last time. A run of Lights pushes the next stroke's accuracy up, and a Strong at the end of the chain hits far harder: Hina's Strong did 25 after three Lights did 4 each, and Kailo's Strong did 41 to finish Boar A. It is a combo system in the Legend of Dragoon sense, and the numbers make the lesson clear without a word of text.

Mark Kailo was still in Wai'Kenpō from last time, where his only element is "WaveGrd." I switched to Lelua for Flame Kick. His max HP dropped from 160 to 130 on the switch, which matches the stance stats: water carries the HP.

Mark Flame Kick on Boar A: "Wild Boar A takes 24 damage. Weak!" One thing looks wrong, though. The 24 popped up over the top boar while the fire burst hit the bottom-left one. That is the same family of problem as the misplaced "Dodge" I flagged last episode.

Mark My mistake on the next block. The meter gives you the time to each blow, and I added the gaps up instead of reading the new number after each press. Two perfects, then a 46-point hit on the third. The narrator gives the fresh time after every press, and I should have read it.

Mark Then Hina had an idea. "Wait. We both have full mana, and we're side by side on the bar." "That means a Team Up. Press right on your commands." "It takes every drop of mana we have. Don't mess up your half." That is a new system taught at the moment it applies, which is the right time.

It costs every point of mana you both have, so it is a big swing.

Design notes

1. Battle words from characters at the right moment: "Hand me a Noni, quick," the Team Up lesson ("We both have full mana, and we're side by side on the bar"), Kailo learning Counter "Since the last boar knocked me flat." These teach systems as they become useful. Praise, large.

2. Team Ups change with the stance pair: Pōhuehue (fire and nature, rhythm then spin), Light Wave (water and light, Simon, a party buff), Tinju Surya (fire and light, Simon then a timed punch). That gives stances a second purpose beyond weaknesses. Praise, large.

3. Stroke chains: Light, Light, then Strong for 35 to 43 is a clear, satisfying rule you learn from the numbers alone. Praise, medium.

4. "Kailo feeds everyone" on the after-fight page makes the cook's identity part of the reward loop. Praise, medium.

5. Lanu's Grove writing: the Sage's "You give to her the way you give to your grandmother," the knees line, and the Haka schism give the island a living faith. Praise, large.

6. The Great Boar is a real puzzle fight, built from rules taught earlier (Truffle heals, burns block healing, stance swaps). That is good structure. But the helpers healing the boss for about 200 each feels punishing without one more hint, and the summons never stop. Medium.

7. Battle Bags are never introduced. A new player will go into fights without their revival item, as I did. One line from Noelani or Hina when the sash first matters would fix it. Large.

8. Fresh water: scooping from a spring is required for the posts and is only in the booklet. Have the Sage or Kailo say it. Large.

9. Field healing: the totem saves but does not heal, and there is no field use of Heal. Springs exist, but the first one is several screens from the village. Medium.

10. The purse cap at 1000 is invisible. Show "Your purse is full" on the victory screen, or tie it to the Lauhala Bag in dialogue. Medium.

11. Element loadout (tiers and bank) is only explained mid-boss, and the field Element screen does not label the bank row "B" or say what it is. Medium.

12. Yes-or-no boxes still start on "No" for harmless things ("Offer Lanu a cup of water?"), while the boss prompt starts on "Fight now," which is right. Small.

13. Combat pacing: common boars hit for 50 to 65, act often, and heal; three-boar fights took several minutes each. For trash fights in the first area this is long. Medium.

14. The palms that block exits now show "chop 1 of 10," a big improvement on last episode's silent presses. Praise, small.

15. Opened chests should look opened. Small.

16. Choice menus now remember the last topic chosen ("highlighted: Is Haka part of Lanu?" on return), which helps with last episode's complaint about replayed topics. Praise, small.

Story and characters

The story this session was mostly the forest and its faith. The stew and the wake did not advance. What did advance is the island's religion: Lanu as "the mother of all living things," offerings given "the way you give to your grandmother," and a hundred-year split between the village priests, who made Haka, "a man from over the sea," into Lanu's sunlight, and the grove people, who walked away to "live with the trees." That reframes Hina, a Māla Priestess with a light stance, and may explain Miki's jab about "your kind." The tone stays warm and dry, with grief under it.

- Kailo: still the heart. "It hits me, I hit back" is a good, plain line for a cook learning to fight. The game keeps making his job his combat identity (Cook, feeding the party).

- Hina: best written still. "I don't eat anything with a face!" in a menu, and her battle coaching ("Don't mess up your half") is sharp without being mean. Her light stance and the Haka schism make me think her faith is the conflict coming.

- The Sage: calm and pointed. He has the best lore lines and a quest.

- The grove villagers: two small people with real voices (the water every morning, the knees).

- Melia: a short hide-and-seek scene. Still the kid who wants in.

- Tandayag na Opon: an imposing boss with a good gimmick. No dialogue.

- Not seen this session: Noelani, Chief Ulu, Nene, Miki, the Lodge.
