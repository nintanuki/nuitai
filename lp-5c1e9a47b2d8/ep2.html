Design notes

1. Hina's in-fight hints are the best teaching in the game. Escape, stance switching, Wave Guard and the Combo each arrived as an answer to a problem I was having, in her voice. Large, praise.

2. The three-parry counter makes enemy turns active play and pays out real damage (31 to 40). Medium, praise.

3. Monster stances that change between fights mean a fight can't be solved once and repeated; my fire plan was wrong for the second boar fight. Medium, praise.

4. Burn as the answer to healers ("is burning and cannot be healed") is elegant and was set up by one line from Hina last episode. It deserves to be stated more plainly once, because I needed two episodes to connect it. Medium.

5. Combos are a highlight: the rhythm minigames are short and readable, and Spring Rain halving fire damage made it a tactical choice, not just a big hit. The greyed menu shows both mana bars, which is clear. Large, praise.

6. Elements appear to cost very little time on the turn bar; Kailo cast Sauté six times in a row while the Taong Tuod never acted. Poison taxing each action balanced it in the next fight, but against an enemy weak to it this looks like a free win. Medium.

7. Healing monsters outpace the party. Boars heal 30 to 37, Taong Tuod heal 44 to 88, while Hina's Heal does 18. Fights became long attrition (8 to 10 minutes) whenever I didn't have burn or Scold. Large.

8. No enemy HP display makes the boss fight a guessing game; with healers and a boss, I can't decide when to commit or retreat. The Seer's Shell exists but sits in the shared bag. Large.

9. Per-character escape is an interesting idea, but it needs one line the first time, and leaving one hero behind can be fatal. Medium.

10. The pouch and shared bag split is learnable now (down to the character column, Give to), but nothing teaches it and the pouch panel shows the wrong character while choosing. Medium.

11. Changing stance in the Element tab outside battle is free and easy; I found it by accident. A hint would help, since it makes the "wrong stance" problem avoidable. Small.

12. The world's background writing is excellent: the knee-praying villager, the water-bringer, the Sage on Lanu and Haka, the innkeeper's "every road in the island runs past my door". Large, praise.

13. The save file named "The Pork Strikes Back" is a charming touch. Small, praise.

14. Many routes north and east are blocked by trees with no sign of why. Three dead ends in one session wore me down. If it's a tool gate, show the obstacle as something a tool would clear. Medium.

15. The Nuno sa Tulay toll choice is good: paying, refusing in Kailo's voice, and backing off are all reasonable. Refusing leads straight into a hard boss with no warning, which is fine, but the game over afterwards cost about fifteen minutes. Small.

16. The Lanu posts tie a healing-spring mechanic to faith in a way that reads as worldbuilding first and mechanic second. Medium, praise.

17. The nap prompt defaults to "No" while save defaults to "Yes". A free heal at home should default to yes. Small.

18. Turn-order preview while hovering a command (Cook pushing Kailo far down the bar, Escape showing Kailo three times) continues to be a great readable tool. Medium, praise.

Story and characters

The story so far is still small and local on purpose, a boar problem and a stew for a wake, but this hour opened the wider island. The forest has a crossroads inn where "every road in the island runs past my door", a grove of people who left the village priests a hundred years ago, and a bridge with a troll who charges tolls. The best new thread is religious: the village priests teach that Haka, "a man from over the sea", is Lanu's sunlight in a man's body, and the grove calls that borrowing. It gives the setting history and a fault line, and it points straight at Hina.

- Kailo (he): still the warm, practical center. "'Ae, ma'am. I'll take my sandals off. ...wait we don't wear sandals." is his voice in one line. Wave Guard, standing in front of Hina, fits him perfectly, and the toll refusal was very him. Melia calls him "big brother", so he is raising his siblings.

- Hina (she): still the best-written character. She runs the battle tutorial in character ("It's only half, don't whine.", "Don't mess up your half."), and "Running isn't losing" followed by "Some things you can't run from" is quietly ominous. She is the devout priestess, she has a sun stance and a nature stance, and the grove's quarrel with the priests makes her trip and her faith the likely story turn.

- Melia (she): Kailo's little sister. "Go get the Great Boar, big brother! Bring me back a tusk!"

- Noelani (she): "What is it, Kailo? I'm elbow deep in something." Kailo calls her "ma'am", which reads as an older relative or guardian.

- The villager by the totem: still on the Callers in Makua who "talk to the dead".

- The Innkeeper (Crossroads Inn): welcoming, a good line about the woods letting go of you.

- The Sage (she, by her speech about Lanu; the game does not say): calm, firm, a little proud of leaving the priests. Gives the five posts quest.

- The grove villagers: the knee one and the water one are small, funny and kind.

- Nuno sa Tulay: a bridge troll with a temper. "No? NO? Then I'll take it out of your hide!" Fun, and very strong.

- Lanu: now defined: "The Akua of the sky and the forests", "the mother of all living things". Her posts mark healing springs.

- Haka: only named so far, a man from over the sea whom the village priests tie to the sun.
