Design notes

1. Teaching through the companion is excellent: Hina's Element page lines and her Combo prompt ("Wait. We both have full mana, and we're side by side on the bar." "That means a Combo. Press right on your commands.") teach at the right moment in her voice. Praise, large.

2. But the Element page lesson arrives late, only once Hina joins. The rows and the bank were confusing for two sessions before that. Move it to the first opening of the page. Medium.

3. Elements as bag items is a good idea that hides itself. Drops feel like loot; nothing says to look in the bag or to teach from there. A line on the victory screen ("Teach it from the bag") would fix it. Medium.

4. The per-stance slot with the "W" corner is compact and clever, and unreadable until the cursor shows the other name. A legend or the full second name on the grid would help. Small.

5. The stance-switching enemy AI (boars and the Nuno sa Tulay answering Kailo's stance) makes stances matter and turns fights into reading. Praise, large.

6. Two healing boars against a solo player is a wall. It sends the right message (get Hina), but the fight lasts six minutes before the player concludes it. A line from Kailo ("They heal faster than I hit") would make the lesson land sooner. Medium.

7. Turn cost of chains is invisible except through the turn order faces. The preview exists; the rule is never said. Players will learn "long chain = bad" by feel. Medium.

8. Cheap weakness elements (Sauté, 1 mark, short turn) were the best tool all session. Good: it rewards reading stances. Watch that it doesn't make plain attacks pointless. Small.

9. Parry lines from Hina ("Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird.") turn a minigame into character. Praise, medium.

10. Combos change with the two stances (Light Wave, then Pōhuehue). Lots of depth; the Simon and rhythm minigames are quick. Praise, medium.

11. Counter text "Kailo dodges X" is wrong and repeats every fight. Small, but very frequent.

12. Water types are a tidy rule (lake = fresh water, sparkle pond = heal, sea = Nori) learned only by touching every tile. A Sage or post line naming the lake would point the player. Small.

13. Sealed exits seen from the wrong side, three of them in one area, with no sign of how to reach them. If the bridge and pier are the way round, the area map could show the route once seen. Medium.

14. The toll bridge choice is a good moment: you can pay, refuse, or leave, and refusing starts a boss fight. But the boss sits one screen past three fights with no healing spot between, and its chain lightning wipes a two-person party fast. A pond or totem before the pier would keep the loss from costing 25 minutes. Medium.

15. Game Over sends you to the title and the last save with no retry. With saves only at totems, this cost a lot. A "retry from the screen" or an autosave on recruitment would help. Medium.

16. Save names as chapter titles ("The Pork Strikes Back") are charming. Praise, small.

17. The shop's 6000-pule Māla Shoyu with Kailo's "I'll have to find soy sauce some other way." is a good way to show a goal and say it's a quest. Praise, small.

18. "You're bad at gardening" then planting with Hina: funny, and ties the boars' damage to the stew ("The boars tore this one up."). Needs one hint that a companion is required. Small.

19. Pork never drops from boars. If it is meant to come from elsewhere, the stew list should say where. Medium.

20. Mid-fight stat growth with green arrows on the victory screen feels good. Praise, small.

21. Dialogue choice cursor returning to the question just asked causes replays. Small.

22. Hens moving off the tile before a press makes eggs a chase. Small.

Review so far

Nuitai is a 16-bit RPG that wants its fights to be read, not ground. Two hours in, the battle system is still the star: a timed parry with lanes, stances that change your element and your HP, enemies that switch stance in answer to yours, cheap elements that reward a correct guess, combos that change with which two stances you're standing in, and skills that are minigames with a real cost. When it clicks, like the second boar fight with Hina where Scold, Sauté and a combo all mattered, it is better than most of the games it borrows from.

The village and its faith are written with care. People talk about their gods the way people do. Hina is the best character so far and the game uses her well, as a teacher and a voice inside the fights.

What doesn't work yet is the in-between. The game hides its rules in places you only find by accident: elements in the bag, water from the lake, planting needs a partner, chains cost time, pork comes from nowhere. Several exits are visible and unreachable with no hint. A solo fight against two healers runs six minutes before you know it's a wall. And a boss behind three fights with no heal point cost me twenty-five minutes. None of that is a deep flaw. It is signposting.

Would I recommend it today? To someone who loves Chrono Cross and SaGa and enjoys working a system out, yes. To a general RPG player, not yet; they would quit at the boars. Add a few lines of direction and a heal point before the bridge and I would recommend it more widely.

Rules I learned

- Elements drop as items. Teach them from Items > shared bag > Elements. They go to the bank row; drag them onto the grid. Each slot holds one element per stance (the corner letter is the other one).

- Rows are mana cost; row 2 is twice row 1.

- Long attack chains cost a lot of time. Cheap elements give quick turns.

- Sauté (fire, 1 mark) does about 22-26 on a weak target. Boars and the forest mushroom take it as weak when in nature stance.

- Skills (Cook, Scold) cost more the better you play, even below zero, and leave you out of breath, even as the first action.

- Cook Poi needs more than one Taro. Heals the party 30 each, served on Kailo's next turn.
