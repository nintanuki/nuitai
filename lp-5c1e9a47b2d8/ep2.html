Design notes

1. Stance pairs choose the Combo: fire plus flower is Pōhuehue (damage vine), water plus flower is Spring Rain ("A warm rain falls over the party," +18 a turn). Found by playing, and it doubles the stance system. Praise, large.

2. Stances change the body, not just the element: Kailo's max HP 130 in fire, 160 in water; Strength and Vitality trade. A cook with a hot and a cool side. Praise, medium.

3. Hina's stances are named for her two faiths, "Māla Hula" and "Shakan Mudras," and Kailo's water stance is "Wai'Kenpō." The battle system carries the island's religious history. Praise, medium.

4. Burn as the answer to healers ("burning and cannot be healed") next to Scold gives two tools for one problem with different costs. Praise, medium.

5. Parry lanes with a counter on a perfect string remain the best thing in the game; one-lane strings play like a rhythm game. Praise, large.

6. Elemental hits skip the parry meter with no explanation. Two episodes in, it is still guessed, not taught. Medium.

7. The east-edge gaps that cannot be reached from inside (first maze, east forest) read as exits and are not. Add a visual cue or a line from Hina. Medium.

8. Choice box for a 100 pule toll that confirm walks straight through; a quarter of the player's money. Put the cursor on "no" or show the choice before the payment line. Small to medium.

9. Lanu's cups: the quest needs a cup and nothing in the world, the quest page or the bag tells you where to get one. Blocks the quest. Medium to large.

10. Command cursor memory plus a double confirm on Element drops you into the grid editor mid-fight. Make the editor need a separate button. Small.

11. Hina's timely coaching (stance box, Wave Guard, Escape when out of mana) is warm and in character ("Don't whine," "Just don't get used to it"). Praise, medium. But Wave Guard was only taught because I misclicked into water stance. Small.

Story and characters

The story barely moved this episode; it was a day of errands, and the people carried it. The stew and the wake are still the clock: the date panel says the 30th of Kau, which I take to be the last day of Summer, the wake day.

- Kailo: practical and polite. I had him pay the bridge troll and keep the deal, because he is a cook going to a wake. He tanks in water and hits in fire, which fits him.

- Hina: the best-written character still. Coaches without softening ("Don't whine," "I won't say it twice"), slips into her own language when it is bad ("Aduh, this is bad. I'm out of mana, and we're both hurt."), and shuts down a flirting merchant ("My hair shines just fine on its own."). Her two stances are her two religions.

- Maika: merchant in the east forest, flirts with Hina, sells 'Awapuhi and 'Awa Tonic. "Aloha! Lost? Everyone who finds me is lost." "A hui hou. Come back if you get hurt."

- Nuno sa Tulay: bridge troll from Philippine folklore. "This is MY bridge, nobody crosses... for free." Greedy and funny.

- Nuno sa Punso: the anthill spirit, met as monsters in straw hats. They read the fight (hardens against magic, speeds up).

- The child on the east island: "I saw a moth up on the mountain as big as a house! Mama says I dreamed it."

- The grove villager: "My knees hurt. I pray anyway. Lanu grew the knees, she can listen to them complain."

Review so far

Nuitai wants to be a small, warm Chrono Cross on one island: a fight system built on rhythm and reading the enemy, wrapped around a village errand, in a world dense with real Pacific and Southeast Asian culture. In fights it gets there more often than not. The parry lanes, the turn bar, mana that comes from motion and the stance system are a real design, and this episode showed me how deep the stances go: they change your body, your element and which Combo you get. Spring Rain falling over the field was a high point. The writing, mostly Hina, is the other high point.

Where it falls down is legibility and navigation. Unparryable hits are still unexplained, stance switching is unclear in and out of battle, walls hide what look like exits, a quest needs a cup the game never mentions, and a 100 pule toll slips through on one button. The world is a good shape once you know it, but finding your way through it is mostly walking into palms.

Would I recommend it today? To someone who loves JRPG battle systems and is patient with a work in progress, yes, for the fights and for Hina. To anyone else, not yet: it needs to tell the player a few more things before it can be called fair.
