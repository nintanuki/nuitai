Design notes

1. Nene's poi quest is excellent. Small ask, built on Kailo's job, pays out a tool, a shop and a change in her ("keeping the house"). Large, praise.

2. Replanting (take two, put one back) turns the hero's dislike of waste into a mechanic, taught by Hina in two lines. Medium, praise.

3. Block buttons that mirror the enemy's strokes (boars A, Aghoy Y, Papanok Y and X) give each monster a rhythm. Good idea, but the change is not announced and a wrong button is silent, so it reads as broken. Add one line the first time and a miss sound for the wrong button. Large.

4. A "catch" block still lets most of the damage through (29, 37 from boars). Only perfect matters, so catch feels like a miss. Small to medium.

5. Stances are a real trade (HP, damage, weakness), but the menus never show what a stance is weak to. One icon on the stance name would do. Medium.

6. The combo is the answer to healing enemies (Aghoy Green Dream and 74-point heals). The game teaches this only through a long stalemate. A hint from Hina when an enemy heals twice would help. Medium.

7. Light strokes with one stamina point kept landing for many presses. Looks like a bug; it lets you chip forever. Medium.

8. Scold scaling cost with performance, including going below zero, is a good risk mechanic. The Select text is honest. Small, praise.

9. Hina's lines change with the fight ("I'm out of mana" vs "Not my turn to Heal"). She feels present. Small, praise.

10. Hina's Heal (+18) is weaker than a Noni Pulu (+30). The healer's only heal feels bad. Medium.

11. The pot UI is clean and the Cook at the inn explains recipe rules in-world. Greyed recipes should say what is missing. Small.

12. Enemies dropping the element they use (Aghoy drop Haste) ties loot to the Loremaster's line about taking Mana from beaten champions. Small, praise.

13. The disguised Nuno sa Punso was a good surprise for one moment; if more monsters hide as NPCs it will make players afraid to talk to people. Small.

14. Totems only in the village; the Crossroads Inn hub has none and the inn does not save. After a Game Over cost 20 minutes, this is the thing I felt most. Large.

15. Hens that step away when approached are a chore. The Boy's "if you are quick" admits it. Small.

16. Twelve boars and no pork, with an NPC saying boars give pork. Either the drop is very rare or a step is missing; both need a nudge. Medium.

17. Thorn Strike sits in the bag with no way to use it. Medium.

18. The fail state for my one Game Over was mostly mine (unneeded fight, healer with no mana), but the silent wrong-button block made it worse. Medium.

19. The north road leading to an inn instead of the Great Boar was a nice reveal of a bigger island, but there is still no pointer to where the Great Boar is. Small to medium.

20. Folklore monsters (Aghoy, Nuno sa Punso) and Hawaiian words side by side give the world a broad Pacific feel. Small, praise.

Story and characters

The story so far is small and local, and that is its strength. A wake tonight, a stew to make, a boar to hunt. This session it widened: the Crossroads Inn, where "every road in the island runs past my door", tells me the village is one corner of a bigger island.

The theme is clearer now: the belongings of the dead pass to the living. Dad's oar, the Eku, last time. Pono's 'O'o this time, from his widow, because "he'd want it used." Kailo is collecting the tools of the men the village lost.

- Kailo: still the straight man, but "'Ae, ma'am. I'll take my sandals off. ...wait we don't wear sandals." is the most human line he has had. "Hm. That smells right. Let's see who eats it first." He is a cook first.

- Hina: sharp, fond, practical. Teaches replanting, rows, and tells him to eat a Noni Pulu, "lunkhead". Her lines react to the fight. Her healing is weak, her Scold is strong. I like her more each session.

- Nene: the heart of the episode. Grief, a craving for her husband's favorite, a gift, and then she is "keeping the house". Turns into the medicine source.

- Noelani: keeps the stew list and the floor clean. Still not told who she is to Kailo.

- Melia: same line, waiting for her tusk.

- Tutori: same speech; harmless.

- The Innkeeper: one good line about the crossroads.

- The Cook at the inn: explains the pot plainly. "Then stand back. Something is about to boil over."

- The Boy at the inn: "Pork comes from boars. I do not go near boars." "Do you want to see my crab?" Funny and useful.

- The Grocer ("OldMan"): no stew items, buys almost nothing.

- Lanu: the field post says "her". First pronoun for Lanu.

Review so far

Nuitai wants to be a small, warm island RPG with a Chrono Cross battle system underneath, where the hero is a cook and the world is built from Pacific words and folklore. After two hours, it mostly is. The village and its people are written with care, the side quest with Nene is the best thing in the game, and the fights have real ideas: a turn bar that shows costs, a stance wheel that decides everything, block meters that mirror each enemy's strokes, a combo that is the right answer to healers.

What does not work yet is how those ideas are taught. The block buttons changed with no word, the stance weaknesses are invisible, the Thorn Strike item does nothing, and the only saves are in the village. Those turned one honest mistake of mine into a 20-minute Game Over. The economy is thin too: no money from animals, a grocer who buys nothing, and a stew list with pork that never drops.

Would I recommend it today? To a patient Chrono Cross fan who enjoys reading a system, yes, and I would tell them to block with the button on the note, not A. To someone new to the genre, not yet. Fix the block feedback, add a save at the inn, and show stance weaknesses, and that changes.
