Design notes

1. Stances change the body: max HP, stats, weakness and resistance, for the party and the monsters (Kailo 140 fire / 170 water; Hina 170 nature / 140 light; boar 190 nature / 150 fire). This is the deepest idea in the game and it clicked for me in the Status screen. Large (praise).

2. Weakness is only learned by getting hit with "Weak!". Kailo's water stance is weak to nature, Hina's nature stance to fire, and nothing on the Status or battle screens says so. One line per stance ("weak to nature") would turn a trap into a choice. Large.

3. Switching stance scales current HP with max HP, with no warning. It nearly killed Hina and did kill her with the burn. Show the new HP before confirming. Medium.

4. Double Combos triggered by two allies with full mana sitting side by side in the turn queue. A clever use of the queue, taught well by Hina in the moment. Spring Rain as a rhythm minigame is fun. Large (praise). But the only Combo so far is defensive and it lights up whenever the bar happens to line up, which nudges you to waste it. Small.

5. The parry meter is the best moment-to-moment system: a clean three-chime run feels great, and Hina's "Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird." is a perfect reward line. Every boar attack I saw was one of two patterns, and one of them always has two blows 0.1 seconds apart, so one hit is always unavoidable. Medium.

6. Burn is the answer to the boars' truffle heal (+37 each time, +19 while asleep) and it is a 20 percent roll on Sauté. In fight 5 three Weak Sautés in a row did not burn and the fight dragged. Raise the chance on a weak hit, or give a sure-burn option. Medium.

7. The temple raising the fallen for free is the right call early on; the problem is that nothing points to it. A line from Hina before she falls, or from Noelani, would fix it. Medium.

8. Per-character battle bags are a good planning layer and are never taught. Small to medium.

9. Tutori, the tutorial kid, is charming and useful ("Grandpa says it's short for something"), but he is the fifth person in a crowded hut I had no reason to revisit. Put him near Kailo's door. Medium.

10. Preemptive strike by punching a monster on the map: big reward (three free turns, 38 to 41 damage, three mana), easy to do, and it makes the visible monsters feel like part of the world. Small (praise).

11. Use-based growth (Hoku: "Swing a lot and you hit harder. Get hit and you get tougher.") with the Status screen showing which stat is closest to rising. Clear and motivating. Small (praise).

12. Cook in battle is a good risk move for a cook hero (costs tempo and mana, ruined if hit), but it consumes the ingredient even when ruined, and there is no hint that the Meialana is the real kitchen until you walk onto the dock. Medium.

13. Lanu's posts give no feedback at the post (no sound, no sparkle); the only reward is "Posts 2 / 5" in a submenu. For an offering to a goddess it feels cold. Small.

14. No save point found in an hour of a village, a temple and the hero's own boat. With fights that can take a careless party down, this makes every risk feel too costly. Large.

15. Navigation: many open edges and paths are blocked by single trees or water with no visual cue (temple north gap, lake north bank). The area map only shows explored rooms. Small to medium.

16. Interactables that do nothing (scroll stones, beach sign, fire pit) look like they should. Small.

17. Writing: the faith worldbuilding (Lanu and Haka, the local priests calling Haka "Lanu's sunlight", no statues of the Lagata, banyans as Bandar's mark) is excellent, and the small lines about Pono from many people build a dead man well. Large (praise).

18. The knocked-out-ally-acts bug (Stuck item 9). Medium.

Review so far

Nuitai is trying to be a warm, small-scale 16-bit RPG about a cook on a Pacific island the day of a funeral, with a battle system that asks you to read and react rather than menu through. After two hours I think it's mostly getting there. The writing is the best thing in it: every villager adds a detail to Old Pono, Hina is a great foil, and the faith history of Lanu and Haka is thoughtful and drawn from real Polynesian and Buddhist ideas without lecturing. The battle system has three genuinely good ideas that fit together: a turn queue with real time costs, parrying by reading blows on a meter, and stances that change your body as well as your element, plus Chrono Trigger style Combos that the queue itself triggers.

What doesn't work yet is teaching and safety. Big rules (battle bags, the temple revive, stance weaknesses, HP scaling on a stance switch, the boat being the kitchen) are found by accident or by talking to the right kid. And there is no save point I can find, so a careless fight can cost an hour. Fights against healing boars run long when the burn chance doesn't land.

Would I recommend it today? To a patient player who likes Chrono Cross style systems and reading every NPC, yes, with a warning to talk to Tutori first and not to switch stances on a low-HP ally. To most players, not yet: I'd want a save point and a stance-weakness readout before I'd hand it to someone.
