Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark Welcome back. Last time ended in a hidden clearing north of the lagoon boardwalks, standing in front of Maika, Miki's runaway brother, who sells medicine. Kailo is at 87 of 150, Hina is full, and I have not saved since the village totem. The studio's notes say Maika now sells the 'Awapuhi, which is on Noelani's stew list, and that selling works like a basket now. So the plan for today: buy the 'Awapuhi, decide what to do about Miki, turn in the ten boars, and look for the giant moth the child on the east bank talked about. I also still have no idea where fish come from.

Mark Here we are. Maika at his stall, and there is a chest in the top right corner of the clearing I do not remember opening. A boar and an Aghoy are wandering the lower half.

Mark Maika's shelf first. The 'Awapuhi is there now at 20, "Wild ginger. Heals one member 5 HP." So the studio's fix works: it is on the shelf, and the description is in plain English. Ti Leaf, "Ti plant leaf. Cures sleep and confusion." 'Ōlena, the one I have been carrying since the forest chest, is "Turmeric. In a fight, cures poison." That answers an old unknown from my notes. The child talked about a giant moth, and moths in games tend to mean poison powder, so I am buying two more 'Ōlena on the strength of that guess.

Mark "Bought 1 'Awapuhi." Good, the line I asked for in Episode 1 is there and reads clearly. One small thing: when the "How many" box was up for the 'Awapuhi, the hook pointer was drawn one row higher, beside 'Ōlena, while the description was the ginger's. It caught my eye because last episode I bought the wrong thing in a shop.

Mark The chest in the corner: "Found Lava Shield." Again, only the name. In Equip it turns out to be a relic, "Resists nature but is weak to water." My headcloth, the Honu Putong, resists light and is weak to shadow. Everything on this island so far has been fire or nature: boars, Aghoy, Papanok in their nature stance, the Great Boar. I have met nothing light or water. So the Lava Shield goes on Kailo. The compare screen with the two little element icons and green and red arrows is quick to read; I like it.

Mark Three Noni Pulu to top Kailo up to 150. The last one only did 3 of its 30, a small waste. That is on me.

Mark The area map tells me the forest is nearly all walked. But the palm maze has an opening on its left side, and the room west of the crossroads has an exit going south, and both point at the same blank square on the map. That is the one place I have not been. The mountain the child mentioned has to be somewhere, and that blank square is my best guess. On the way I will turn the ten boars in at the lodge.

Mark Two Aghoy caught me leaving the clearing. The parry meter is the new name, and I got three perfect presses in a row: the meter says "PERFECT" and a "PARRY" tag pops up beside the party. Two different words for one press is a little odd, but both read as good news. Flame Kick does 64 on an Aghoy, "Weak!". Hina's strokes finished the first, then I turned her last point on the second one without backing out, and the leftover went with her as the notes promised.

Mark Three more perfects on the second Aghoy's blows at Hina, and "Hina dodges Aghoy B." Then Flame Kick for 60 and a Medium stroke to finish. Kailo got three turns in a row in the order, which I take as his Agility going up; the after-fight screen says "PER up, AGI up". A Noni Pulu dropped. Easy fight, as last episode.

Mark Another cup of water at a lanu post on the way out: "Kailo poured Lanu a cup of water." Nothing visible happens. I keep doing it because Kailo does, and the game keeps a count of something, I suspect. A lone boar, Flame Kick for 66, done.

Mark Back on the lagoon boardwalks. The chest on the boardwalk reads "It's empty." It looked closed from a distance, the same as the ones I have not opened, so either I emptied it last time or it was a decoy. Three more chests sit out in the open water where no plank reaches. Those are a promise: a boat, a swim, or a bridge later.

Mark Two Nuno sa Punso on the planks. One opened with "Haunted Glow" on Kailo for 43, no parry meter, so that is an element or a Skill. Flame Kick on them did 34 and 30, no "Weak!", so they are not nature. I noticed the "Needs 2 mana." box when I miscounted my strokes and tried Flame Kick with 1; it showed, and I backed out, took another stroke, and kicked. That box is quick and clear. Six perfect parries this fight. The parry meter has become second nature, and it is the most fun part of a plain fight.

Mark This is the fourth fight in a row that is no threat. I am spending about two minutes per fight on monsters that cannot hurt me. That is a sign I have outgrown this forest.

Mark So when two more Nuno sa Punso caught me on the planks, I tried the new Escape. You choose it, and a bar says "Alternate!"; you rock L and R at an even beat. I kept a steady rhythm and got "Escaped." first try. That is a good change. It makes running a small skill instead of a dice roll, and it is quick enough that I will actually use it on fights I have outgrown. The child on the east bank still says the same thing about the moth "as big as a house", and that her mama says she dreamed it.

Mark Through the islet, the east wood, and the palm maze's left side, into the blank square on the map: a room with stumps and boars and an exit on the west. And at that exit, Hina stops me: "Kailo, wait! That's the deep forest. Lanu's beasts live in there, and they're much stronger than anything out here. And it's easy to get lost." That is exactly the kind of line I said was missing in Episode 3. It tells me there is somewhere harder to go without walling it off.

Mark Then she adds, "Besides, the Great Boar is north. You're going the wrong way." And Kailo says "...right." The Great Boar has been dead for three episodes. This line has not caught up with the story. It is a small thing, but it is the kind of thing that breaks the spell, because Hina is the character who keeps track of everything.

Mark The warning did not stop me, so I took it as a choice. Two stumps jumped me while she was talking; I escaped again, cleanly, and stepped west.

Mark The deep forest. A sandy crossroads with a little pond and a lot of dense palms. Paths run west, north and south. Still called Kulāiwi Forest on the narrator's side. I want to see one of these "much stronger" beasts before I decide whether we belong here.

Mark Kailo scooped fresh water from the pond, which restocks Poi. Then west, and west again, and north. Every room is the same: sandy paths between thick palms, no monsters walking, no chests, no people. I opened the area map to keep my bearings and the deep forest is not drawn on it at all, and there is no dot for Kailo. So "easy to get lost" is literal: the map stops helping the moment you step in. I like that in principle, Zelda's Lost Woods does the same, but the Lost Woods teaches you its rule with music and a reset. Here, so far, I cannot tell whether I am getting lost or getting somewhere.

Mark That room north of the second crossroads was a dead end: a big empty clearing with nothing in it. The next room north of the west road is another dead end. Six rooms in, and not one of Lanu's "much stronger" beasts. I expected at least one fight to tell me whether I am ready for this place.

Mark I was stuck enough to open the booklet for the first time this session. What I wanted: anything on the deep forest, how to find my way, or where the mountain is. I searched it for "deep forest" and "lost". The only hits were the reset combination and the bow's Banish Skill. So the booklet does not cover this place. Fair enough; it is a manual, not a guide.

Mark I mapped it in my head instead. West of the pond room, then west again to a crossroads; that crossroads goes north to a dead end and west to another room; that room goes north to a dead end and south to a third; and the third room's east exit drops me back into the second. So it is a small square of four rooms with two dead-end spurs and a dead-end corridor. No beasts, no chest, no way on that I could find. Either something opens it later, an item or a time of day, or I am missing a path. With no map and no landmarks, I cannot tell which, and that is the part that frustrates me. A deep forest that warns of strong beasts and then shows none feels unfinished rather than mysterious.

Mark I walked back out the way I came, through the palm maze and the crossroads, and saved at the Lanu's Grove totem. Same title, "The Pork Strikes Back", now at Lanu's Grove with 809 shells. That was the right call: I had not saved since the village.

Mark A grove villager I had not heard before: "I bring Lanu water from the spring every morning. It is hers anyway, but she likes being asked." That one line explains the lanu posts better than any tutorial would. The cups of water I keep pouring are not a mechanic, they are manners. I like that a lot.

Mark Then the long walk down to the lodge to turn in the ten boars. On the way, two Aghoy, and I pressed one blow early on purpose to see the new partial parry. The meter says nothing, the tag beside the party says "Glancing", and Hina took 4 instead of the full blow. So the words are: PERFECT on the meter and PARRY on the party for a dead-center press, "Glancing" for an edge press. That reads clearly. Then Escape again, and again it worked. A boar pair in the east wood, same.

Mark In the lodge I walked into Gajumaru's table by accident, and since I was there I asked him where he is from. "Kami Kuku... the Kame Kingdom...", "Far... to the northwest... green islands... clear springs...", "Great turtles... swim there... older than... anyone...", "We heal... the sick... with what... the springs give..." And how he got here: a Bandari trader from Kota Emas, his friend Gin'imun, a ship that "was fast... too fast...", a trader who "sold... his goods... and left... without us...", and Gin'imun who "swam on... to Māla Iki...". Kailo: "Your friend just left you here?" Gajumaru: "I was... still... thinking... about my move..." That is a lovely joke told at a turtle's speed. It is also a lot of world in one conversation: a turtle kingdom to the northwest, a trading people, and Māla Iki again, which is where Miki comes from. Then I pressed one A too many and sat down to a game of Kaigara; I resigned at once ("You resigned. Gajumaru wins.") and he said, "The shells... were kind... to me... today..."

Mark One of the Hunters now says, "A Seer's Shell tells you what a beast is made of, and how much fight it has left." That is the line I asked for in Episode 3, the one that would have pointed me at the Shell before the Great Boar. It is in the right place, in a hunters' lodge, said by a hunter.

Mark The Trapper: "That's the lot. Here's your 150 pule, and a Seed of Strength." "Got 150 pule and a Seed of Strength." Then the next bounty: "Bounty: five sigbin. Pays 80 pule and a Seed of Agility." I took it. I have never seen a sigbin. So there is a monster on this island I have not met, and my best guess is that the deep forest is where it lives, and that I did not find it because I did not go deep enough or it only comes out at some other time.

Mark That is thirty minutes. I am in the lodge, saved at Lanu's Grove before the walk down, with the 150 pule and a Seed of Strength unsaved. Next time I want to find the sigbin, which should finally show me what the deep forest is for, get the moth's mountain located, and bring Noelani the 'Awapuhi and whatever else the bag now covers. I also want to try Kaigara properly; Gajumaru has earned a real game. See you then.

Design notes

1. Escape as an L/R rhythm is a clear improvement: quick, a little skill, and it worked every time with a steady beat. It makes running from fights I have outgrown practical. Praise, medium.

2. Hina's deep-forest warning is the kind of in-world signpost I asked for: it says where the hard part of the island is without walling it off. Praise, large. But her line about the Great Boar being north is out of date three episodes after his death, and from the character who tracks everything it breaks the spell. Medium.

3. The deep forest promises strong beasts and shows none in seven rooms, has no map and no landmarks, and loops. Without a single fight or a single feature, being lost there feels like an empty place, not a puzzle. Either a beast near the entrance (to make the warning true) or one landmark per room (to make navigation learnable) would fix it. Large.

4. The new Hunter line about the Seer's Shell is in the right place and in the right voice. It answers my Episode 3 note exactly. Praise, medium.

5. The grove villager's "she likes being asked" line explains the lanu posts as manners rather than a mechanic. Best world-building line this episode. Praise, medium.

6. Gajumaru's backstory (Kame Kingdom, Bandari trader, Gin'imun, "still thinking about my move") is funny and dense with places. Praise, medium. His slow typing is a character choice, but with five or six pages and a menu that reopens, it is easy to press into a game by mistake. Small.

7. The parry meter rename works: "Glancing" for an edge press is clear, and PARRY reads as success. The meter's own "PERFECT" alongside "PARRY" is one word too many. Small.

8. Maika's shop now sells the 'Awapuhi with an English description, and "Bought 1 'Awapuhi." confirms. The studio's fix works. The hook pointer drawn one row off during "How many" is a small visual bug. Small.

9. Opened chests look the same as unopened ones at a glance (the boardwalk chest). The Lava Shield chest did look darker afterwards, so maybe this one was a decoy; either way, I could not tell. Small.

10. Chests in open water are a good promise of a later tool. Praise, small.

11. The "Needs 2 mana." box on a greyed Flame Kick is quick and right. The studio's fix works. Praise, small.

12. Plain fights in this forest take about two minutes each and pose no threat. Escape helps, but the island has nothing left to teach in combat until the sigbin shows up. Medium.

13. The Lava Shield's compare screen, two element icons with green and red arrows, is fast to read. Praise, small. "Found Lava Shield." still says nothing until the menu. Small (same as before).

14. The Trapper's turn-in and new bounty in two lines, with the reward named, is clean. A bounty for a monster I have never seen ("sigbin") is a good hook. Praise, small.

Story and characters

The story is still in its first-island stretch: the boar is beaten, the stew list is shrinking, and the hooks are all pointing off the island. Today added the deep forest, a bounty for a monster I have not met, and a lot of geography from Gajumaru: a turtle kingdom to the northwest, the Bandari traders from Kota Emas, and Māla Iki again. Māla Iki is now connected to Miki, to the Māla Shoyu on the stew list, to Hina's title, and to Gajumaru's friend Gin'imun. I think it is the next island.

- Kailo: quiet today. "...right." when Hina corrects him is in character: he does not argue with her. "Your friend just left you here?" is the most he said, and it lands, given his own father.

- Hina: still the guide. Her deep-forest warning is good writing: protective, practical, a little bossy. The Great Boar line is the one wrong note.

- Maika: same as before; "A hui hou. Come back if you get hurt." He is the medicine seller now in practice.

- Gajumaru: the best scene today. Slow, warm and funny; abandoned by a trader while thinking about his move, and not bitter about it. He is a "he" by the game's words.

- The grove villagers: still one line each and still all good. Lanu as a goddess you ask politely is consistent across them.

- The Trapper: business only, as before. Now hunting sigbin.

- The child on the east bank: same line; the moth "as big as a house" and "Mama says I dreamed it".
