Design notes

1. The Seer's Shell, once found, solves my old complaint (enemy HP and stance not on screen): "Papanok B: Fire and Light, in Fire now. HP 74/80, mana 6." But it sits in a "Special" bag category and the shop sells it for the most, so a new player can sell it without knowing. Medium.

2. The Combo is too strong for the early game: 150 to 208 damage against enemies with 80 to 190 HP, and always one-shotting. After the first two fights every fight is "charge mana, line up, press right". Large (for balance).

3. Defend is a quiet tool of many uses: halves damage, skips the burn tick, adds one mana, and slides the turn order so Kailo and Hina line up for a Combo. I found it by accident. It deserves one line of teaching. Small, praise.

4. The turn-order queue on the right carries the whole Combo rule, but only the narrator spells out the adjacency; on screen the player sees only a grayed command. Medium.

5. Burn plus no battle-bag cure punishes the player a second time (Episode 4, again here). A Kukui in the main Medicine list does nothing in a fight. Large.

6. A parry that lands also counters (27 and 38 damage popped up on a boar). That is a good reward for the parry and I had not noticed it. Praise.

7. Boars that heal each other (truffle thrown, +30; asleep, +19) and shift stance make the two-boar fights long unless the player has a Combo ready. They also give no pule. The overworld road to the Nuno is eight to ten fights each way. Medium.

8. Healing options are far from the road: the totem raises the fallen, the bed and the pond heal. A single pond near the bridge would shorten each loop. Medium.

9. Praise: the writing lands again. Hina's "Aduh! Wh-what are you doing?! That's my bed! Get away from it!" and Noelani's "I did her chores again. She owes me." make the house feel lived in.

10. Praise: the sell menu showing "-" for items the grocer will not buy is clear, and the quantity prompt (right adds ten) is quick.

11. The Paddle skill is hidden behind a name; the menu should say "mash". Small.

Review so far

This is a game that wants me to learn by dying and then rewards me for learning, and the parts that reward me are very good: the turn order with real time costs, the parry meter with its counter, the stance changes, and a Combo that feels like two people actually working together. The writing is the strongest part, and it was nice to see it again in Noelani and Hina's bed. What does not work yet is everything that is hidden from the player: the Combo's adjacency rule, burn and its missing cure, the Seer's Shell in the shop, the water for the Lanu post, how to get the digging stick, the bag's shoulder buttons. The roads are respawning fights that cost minutes with nothing paid for them, and the Combo is so strong it flattens the middle of every fight. I would recommend it to a patient player who likes learning rules the hard way and loves writing, and I would hold off for someone who wants to be guided. I am closer to a yes than in Episode 6: I found a cheap way to make 121 pule, I know what the Seer's Shell is for, and I finally crossed the bridge.
