Design notes

1. The Nuno sa Punso pay back the toll. One gave about 85 pule; a three-Nuno fight gave 255. After seven episodes of scraping for 100, this is the first time money felt like a reward. Praise, medium.

2. Parry finally clicks once you trust the number. Five and seven blow strings all parried by reading the meter fresh each time; each parry also hits back. This is the best system in the game. Praise, large. But a blow string with gaps under 0.15 s is unparryable by design and nothing tells you that. Small.

3. The Combo rule (partner before the next enemy) is learnable but invisible. Show a small link between the two faces in the queue when a Combo is available. Medium.

4. Three hasted enemies make the eight-face queue unreadable: my own fighters drop off it. A longer queue or a "next turn in N" marker would help. Medium.

5. Battle bags you cannot read from the field lead to forgetting what you carry. Two Noni Pulu sat unused while Hina died. Show the bag's contents on the Items page. Large, because it cost a fall.

6. Stance changes for defence work beautifully when you see a "Weak!": switching Hina to nature cut a 23 to a 10. But the weaknesses are learned only by being hit, and one hit (56) can kill. A first-time "Weak!" that one-shots the healer is a harsh teacher. Large (still, from earlier episodes).

7. The Life Totem's text and behavior disagree ("Used in a fight." but usable in the field). The behavior is the better one; fix the text. Small.

8. The element-teaching scrolls and the locked third slot have no explanation on screen; a padlock and "beat a boss to carry more moves" would do it. Small.

9. A lone Green Aghoy that revives itself with Green Dream and resists both stances has no counter the player can find here. Either a visible counter (burn, silence) or a cap on revives. Medium.

10. The grove behind the trees is a strong lure, but it has now been visible and untouchable for two episodes, with no hint of the tool. Large (still).

11. The Nuno sa Tulay should step aside after you pay, and "Fight?" should open on No. As it is, paying looks like it did nothing and one stray press starts the boss fight. Medium.

12. Writing: Ama's new lines (black sails, the stranger in boots, the whirlpool "like a spoon stirring a pot", wayfinding by swell, stars and terns, boat Mana, "Half the boats in this harbor came off your dad's hands") are the best writing in the game so far, and they plant the plot. Praise, large.

13. The shoe joke paying off as the mark of a foreigner ("In boots! Nobody on these islands even owns shoes.") is good setup and payoff. Praise, small.

14. The two spellings (spoken Hawaiian-shaped, Lore page in another form) are consistent and interesting; a line on the Lore page linking them would stop it reading as a typo. Small.

15. Noelani as a bank and a wallet upgrade gives the household a role in the economy. Praise, small.

16. Kailo's bed and the totem remain the only full resets, far from the fights past the toll. This side of the bridge has no pond I can reach and no save. Medium (still).

17. The Smoke Bomb ("The whole party flees a fight at once.") is the right tool for a fight that cannot be won; Escape's one-at-a-time mash is not. Praise, small.

Review so far

Nuitai is a small-island RPG about a cook and a priestess on the last day of Summer, built on a time-cost turn queue, stances that change your body, and a parry meter that rewards reading the screen. Today it felt like a game I'm good at rather than one I'm surviving. The parry system is excellent once you trust the meter. The Combo, when it lands, is the most satisfying hit I've had in a while. The Nuno sa Punso finally made money a reward. And the writing, Ama's especially, keeps getting better and has started to tell a real story. What doesn't work is still mostly hidden information: the Combo rule, what is in your battle bag, which stance is weak to what until it kills you, the locked element slot, the tool that opens the trees, and a toll troll who doesn't step aside. Hina has died to single unseen hits too often. I'd recommend it today to patient players who like learning systems by poking at them and who read every line of dialogue. I'd still hold off recommending it to anyone who wants the game to tell them where to go or why a button is gray.
