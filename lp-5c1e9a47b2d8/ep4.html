Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark Welcome back. Last time ended at the totem in Lanu's Grove, right after a save. Kailo and Hina are at full health, I have the Ko'i adze for chopping trees, 970 pule, and a fair stack of Noni Pulu. The Great Boar is still the main goal, the wake stew is still mostly unmade, and Maika turned me back from the Lost Woods.

Mark My plan for this hour: take the Ko'i to the two tree-blocked spots I noted, the chest by the river in Kulāiwi Forest and the top exit of the palm room west of the crossroads. One of them may be the real road up the mountain. If both are side pockets, I go back to Maika and see whether anything has changed about the Lost Woods. I also want to find out where Lanu's cups come from, since two posts are waiting on one.

Mark South out of the grove. The sign says "Kulāiwi Forest", and this is the crossroads with the inn, but it is busier than I left it: two Taong Tuod, an Aghoy, and two of the straw-hat Nuno, one standing by the side chest I already emptied and one beside a chest in the lower right that I do not remember at all. The lower-right one is worth a look later. I want the west room first.

Mark The palm room west of the crossroads. Last time the top exit was walled off by palms. Now I can see the line: up the middle, and one palm sits right in the gap at the top. I walk up under it and swing the Ko'i.

Mark First swing, and a boar I had walked right past hits me from behind. "Ambush!" That is fair. I turned my back on a visible monster to chop wood.

Mark Three perfect blocks to open, and the counter comes in for 25. The parry timing feels good now; the gold zone is generous enough that I can hit dead center on purpose.

Mark One of the boars "uses Nap", "curls up" and sleeps, and the other "shifts to fire stance". Same pattern as before. I open on the awake one with a Light stroke for 7, then Strong for 48 and Strong again for 42. Light first really does pay for itself: it lifts the Strong stroke's hit chance.

Mark Hina's turn. Fire boar, flower-stance Hina: the lesson from last time says get her out of flower. I open her Element list to switch and it shows her other stance is called "Shakan Mudras", with one element in it, "ShdGrd". The preview box above the list is empty again, the same blank window I flagged last episode. A shadow guard does nothing about fire, so I back out and have her hit with the staff instead: 24, 25, 4, and the game says "Wild Boar B falls."

Mark Sauté on the sleeper wakes it ("It woke up.") for 14, then it "shifts to nature stance" and my next Sauté reads "Weak!" for 27, and it heals 37 right back. That is the old problem: a boar's Heal outpaces my fire spell. Strong strokes do 48 and 41, which is better than Sauté even against a weakness. I am starting to think elements here are for status and stance, and strokes are for damage.

Mark Three more perfect blocks, another counter, and one Strong stroke from Kailo finishes it. "Wild Boar A falls." Victory, a Noni Pulu, 30 pule, and "Kailo's Endurance up!"

Mark One thing confused me in that fight. The game said "Wild Boar B falls" well before the end, yet a boar stayed on the field the whole time, and the last one was called A when it fell. The narrator kept calling them both "standing". I could not tell which was which from the screen; they look identical and there is no letter over them.

Mark "Kailo feeds everyone." HP ticks up to 139 and 176. Still a nice touch. Then back to the palm: ten chops for the tree and five for the stump, same as last time, and the gap at the top is open.

Mark Through the gap, and it is a new room. Still called "Kulāiwi Forest". Rows of palms, two Taong Tuod, two Papanok, a pond in the lower right, and a Lanu post beside it.

Mark The post: "A post for Lanu. I need a cup of water to leave her an offering." So that is three posts I have found that want a cup and one I watered. I still have no idea where cups come from, and there is a pond right next to this one. If the pond could fill a cup, I would expect the game to say so here. A post sitting beside water and refusing the water is a strong hint that the cup is an object I am missing, not an action.

Mark The top exit is behind another solid row of palms, so I start walking around the long way, and walk straight into a Papanok. Three of them. Last time three Papanok killed Hina with "Weak!" Sautés, so this time I plan for it before anyone moves.

Mark Hina goes first, and I switch her out of the flower stance into the other one, "Shakan Mudras". Her HP cap drops from 200 to 160 in it. Then Kailo goes to Wai'Kenpō, the water stance, which takes his cap from 160 to 200. Both switches are shown up front in the max HP, which I like: the stat change is right there in the number.

Mark The switch preview box is empty again, the blank rounded window above the move list. I am now fairly sure it is meant to hold the stance's name.

Mark It paid off. Papanok Sauté on water Kailo: "Kailo takes 8 damage. Resisted." On Hina in the new stance, 19, not "Weak!". Last episode the same move did 35 to 51 to her. Flambé does 5 or 6 to Kailo and 13 to 15 to her. This is the stance system working the way I hoped: I read the enemy's element, I set my stances against it, and the fight goes from deadly to comfortable.

Mark Blocking feels sure now. I go nine for nine perfect across three enemy turns, and two of the counters land for 40. One small wording thing: after three perfect blocks the text says "Kailo dodges Papanok C." I blocked, I did not dodge, and the screen showed a block.

Mark Two Papanok down to stroke chains. Then Kailo and Hina line up on the turn bar with full mana, and the Combo page offers something new: "Light Wave", water and the sun symbol. It is a lights game, Simon Says: the game plays a string on the four face buttons and I press it back, one longer each round. The bar keeps draining while the game is showing the string, which feels unfair: the clock should stop while I am only allowed to watch. I got through three rounds and fumbled a double press.

Mark "Kailo and Hina: Light Wave!" "A wave of light settles over the party." Gold specks float over the field and both of us get a "wave" tag. It used all our mana. What it does, I cannot tell: no number, no text after. The next Sauté still did 8 to Kailo and 19 to Hina, the same as before. A support combo that does not say what it supports is a hard sell when it costs all the mana two people have.

Mark Then my own mistake. Hina was at 34 and I looked in Kailo's battle pouch for medicine: only a Kukui. I backed out, pressed up once too many, and the cursor wrapped from Attack to Item, and my next confirm used the Kukui on nothing. That is on me, but the wrap is quick and silent. Kailo then finished the last Papanok with a 38 and a 39.

Mark Drops: three Eggs and a "Seed of Agility". No pule from roosters. Hina got Endurance and Vitality up and her cap went to 180. And while looking in the bag I find a Coconut I never picked up knowingly. I think the palm I chopped dropped it. That is one of the three Coconuts on the stew list. If palms give Coconuts, the Ko'i is part of the stew quest too, which is neat.

Mark I fed Hina three Noni Pulu from the shared bag, 52 to 172, and moved two Noni Pulu into Kailo's own pouch so that his battle Item list is not empty again. The Use and Give to box appeared over the bag the way I remembered.

Mark I go around the long way to the top row, and one Papanok steps into me on the way. One rooster alone is easy now: a stroke chain, three perfect blocks, another chain, done. An Egg. Then ten chops and five more on the top row, and up.

Mark And there it is. The music changes to something heavy, and the room is a wide clearing with patches of dug-up dirt, seven ordinary boars, and in the middle, a huge black boar with a spiked mane and a crooked tail. The narrator gives its name as "Tandayag na Opon". The game itself has not named it on screen yet. My prediction that the Great Boar is real and lives at the top of the woods looks right, though I want to hear the game call it that before I mark it.

Mark One of the small boars spots me (a red "!") and charges before I can think. Two boars. Hina strokes for 55, Kailo for 88, I block everything, one curls up for a nap, and it falls before it wakes. One odd moment: a "Dodge" popped over the boar while it was asleep. A sleeping animal should not be able to dodge. The other one goes down to a 43. A Pork, and Hina's "Luck up!"

Mark Now the honest question. The boss is right there, I am not at full health, and my last save is three rooms back in the grove. If I lose, I could lose the two trees I chopped and every fight since. Any careful player walks back and saves. The Sage also said "Wherever you find her post with a cup beside it, there is a spring near that heals", and there is a post and a pond one room down, so I will test that on the way.

Mark The pond heals. "The party drinks from the cool pond. Everyone is rested." So the Sage's rule holds: a Lanu post with water beside it means a healing spring. That is good teaching. One line from a minor character, and three rooms later it pays off.

Mark Now back toward the grove to save. In the crossroads two Taong Tuod catch me. Kailo goes back to Lelua for fire, since stumps in nature stance are weak to it.

Mark The stumps are the same slog I remember. Sleep All puts us both down for a round, Heal for 44 and then 88, Poison and a new one, "Blind". And then something I am fairly sure is a bug. My strokes knocked Taong Tuod A down. The game showed it as finished, there was no "falls" message, and on its next turn it used Heal on itself for 88 and stood back up, then did it again. Later, after a Sauté finally set it burning, it took its turn and cast Blind on Hina before the burn tick finished it and the text said "Taong Tuod A falls." An enemy at zero should not get to act. And when it did fall, the burn number, 18, popped over the other stump, not the one fading out.

Mark The burn did what burn does: "Taong Tuod A catches fire", and its heals stopped mattering. Burn is still the best idea in this combat system, and it is still too random to plan around. Two "Weak!" Sautés did not light, the third did.

Mark Kailo was blinded, so strokes were a bad bet. Kailo in fire and Hina in her sun stance lined up with full mana, and the Combo page offered "Tinju Surya". It starts with the same lights game as Light Wave, which I failed outright this time: the first light came and went while I was still reading, and the string ran out on me. Then a second part: a circle shrinks on the enemy, "Ready...", and one tap throws a punch, harder the smaller the circle. I threw it at 14 percent. "Taong Tuod B takes 289 damage. Weak!" One hit, gone.

Mark That is a strong combo even played badly, and the punch half is the best minigame I have seen here: one clear target, one press, the size of the circle tells you how well you did. The lights half has the same problem as before. The clock runs while the game is still showing the string, and the first light can pass before you know the game has started.

Mark Drops: a Life Totem, two Noni Pulu, a Taro. Strength up for both.

Mark Back at the grove totem. "The party kneels at the totem and tells the Akua all that has happened." Saved. The slot is titled "The Pork Strikes Back", which made me smile; I take it the save name follows the chapter or the last big event. The confirmation, "Saved to slot 1.", sits at the bottom of the slot screen in yellow. Fine.

Mark While I am here, I ask the Sage about cups, since that is my one open puzzle. The Sage greets me with "Lanu's grove. Speak softly; the trees are listening. Ask, if you came to ask." and offers four topics. "Why the cups of water?" gets: "Lanu is given what feeds her children. Water for the roots, a little taro now and then." "She does not need it. That is not the point. You give to her the way you give to your grandmother." That is a lovely line. But it explains why, not how. Every post I find makes Kailo say he needs a cup of water, and no one tells me where a cup comes from. I have watered one post, so I know it can be done; I just cannot remember how, and the game is not reminding me.

Mark Now the Great Boar. I keep Kailo in Lelua and Hina in her sun stance, because the fire and sun pair is the one that gave me the 289 punch, and every boar so far has spent most of its time in nature stance.

Mark The walk back costs three fights: a lone boar, then two pairs of Papanok in the pond room. The roosters have come back since last time. With Kailo in fire and Hina in sun, Flambé does 6 and 7 to us, and every stroke chain is a kill or close to one. Drops: a Seer's Shell twice, Eggs, a Noni Pulu, another Seed of Agility. Respawning fights on a road I have to walk three times is starting to wear, but they are short now that my stances are right.

Mark The pond room's "braces for the blow" Papanok taught me one more thing: when an enemy braces, my strokes do half or less. A Light for 2 and a Medium for 14. That is the enemy's Defend, and it is readable, so I should hit the other one when I see it.

Mark In the clearing, three boars jump me ("Preemptive strike!", so I got the first move this time) and go down in a couple of minutes. Pork twice more; I now have far more Pork than the stew needs.

Mark Then I walk up to the big one, and Hina stops me: "That's him. Are you ready?" And the box: "Fight the Great Boar now?" with "Fight now" and "Go back". So the game does call it the Great Boar. Prediction four is right: Melia's legend was true, the Great Boar is real and it sits at the top of the woods. Its narrator name, Tandayag na Opon, I have still not seen on screen.

One change, and that's your turn." I like that it is taught at the moment it matters, right before a boss. Design notes

1. Large, praise: stances finally clicked. Setting Kailo to water and Hina to sun before Papanok turned "Weak!" Sautés of 35 to 51 into 8 and 19. Reading the enemy and setting stances is the best decision loop in the game.

2. Large, praise: the Great Boar is a fair final exam for the forest. It heals, summons healers, and the answer, burn, is the one Hina taught in Episode 1. Winning felt earned.

3. Large: summoned boars crowd the turn bar (six of eight slots), so the party never lines up for a second combo, and the fight ran about fifteen minutes. Cap the helpers at two, or end the fight when the boss falls.

4. Medium, praise: Tinju Surya's punch is the best minigame yet: one circle, one press, the size tells you how well you did. 289 and 294.

5. Medium: the lights game drains its timer while the string is still being shown. Freeze it until the player's turn, and flash a clear "Watch" before the first light.

6. Medium: Light Wave and Counter give no readable feedback. A support move needs a line saying what it did ("damage halved", "Kailo kicks back for 9").

7. Medium, praise: the Sage's "post with a cup beside it means a spring near that heals" paid off three rooms later at the pond. One line, one reward.

8. Medium: cups for Lanu's posts are never explained in this session. Three posts want a cup; no hint where one comes from.

9. Medium: element descriptions with numbers ("15 physical fire, 20% burn.") are excellent, but they live only in the field Element tab. Show them in battle when the cursor sits on an element.

10. Medium: the after-victory confirm should not be able to skip a "slot unlock" screen. Make new unlocks need their own press, or hold them for a beat.

11. Medium: the fallen stay down after a win, against Hina's join text. Either fix it or change the text; I used a Life Totem each time.

12. Small, praise: Hina's mid-boss lesson on swapping elements is taught at the right moment in her voice.

13. Small, praise: Hina's "That's him. Are you ready?" and the "Fight now / Go back" box give a clear boss threshold.

14. Small: respawning fights on the only road between the save point and the boss (eight fights for two trips) wear thin, even though they are short.

15. Small, praise: the Ko'i keeps paying: two palms opened the whole route to the boss, and (I think) a Coconut for the stew.

16. Small: the remembered battle cursor and the wrap from Attack to Item cost me two cure items. Reset the cursor to Attack at the start of each fight.

17. Small, praise: "Kailo feeds everyone" after each fight is still a lovely touch.

Story and characters

The forest arc is done: Melia's legend was true, the Great Boar ruled the top of the woods and sent the little ones down, and Kailo beat it. The game calls it the Great Boar in the question box and Tandayag na Opon in battle. Its name and the Boar Tusk drop tie straight back to Melia's "Bring me back a tusk!", which is good setup paying off. Kailo's lines after the fight, "The taro's safe" and "the wake won't be the same without it", keep him what he has been since the start: a cook whose heroics are about feeding people. The stew is still the spine of the day.

Kailo: steady and likable. "It hits me, I hit back." is a nice bit of growth from the boy who lost alone in the forest.

Hina: still the best voice in the game. "Since when?" is two words and a whole relationship. Her tutorial lines stay in character. Her other stance, Shakan Mudras, and her sun symbol make me think her priest training is the sun, Haka, which fits the Sage's story.

The Sage: generous and dry. "You give to her the way you give to your grandmother." is the best line about Lanu so far, and it makes the grove's religion feel lived in.

Noelani: offstage, but Kailo's "Noelani's waiting on the makings" keeps her at the center of the house.

Melia: offstage, and the tusk is for her.

The Great Boar: a good boss design, a good legend, no lines. I expected a moment after it fell: a look, a cry, the other boars scattering. It just faded.
