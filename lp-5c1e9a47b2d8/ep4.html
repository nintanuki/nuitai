Design notes

1. The Combo (Tinju Surya) is the best thing in the game: both mana bars full and adjacent in the turn order, a four-round lights game, then a timed circle. 222 to 247 on a 12% circle, in one hit. It rewards setup and then a clean execution. Praise. Fix: say why it is gray (small to medium).

2. Parry counters are great and unexplained. A clean parry of all seven Lights popped 27 to 28 on the enemy, and a boar and a Papanok died from counters alone. I found out because the numbers popped over the enemy. It makes the defense part of offense. Praise; one line in the tutorial would help (small).

3. The Hina revive after a win ("Up, all of you. I'm not carrying anyone home.") is the best moment of in-battle writing and it is mechanically important: a knocked-out Kailo is recoverable if Hina wins. It pays off the pop-up from episode 1. Praise. But it only works if Hina survives, and Hina can't revive herself (medium design hole: a Hina knockout with Kailo alive is much worse than the reverse).

4. Burn blocking healing, with 14 per action, is lethal and silent. The one item that stops it (Kukui) must be in a battle bag already. The cure is far from the moment you need it. Medium to large. Fix: mark burned characters on the item target screen; show a burn icon with a number on the field menu; drop the Kukui in battle bags automatically when found.

5. No save point after four episodes, two Game Overs. A permadeath-like loss of 50 minutes at the end of a fight that was winnable is not a design decision I can defend. Large. Even a save at the Meialana would fix it.

6. The Bestiary shows HP and stances and moves, with question marks for the ones I haven't seen, and a sentence of lore. That is very good, and solves "enemy HP and stance are not on screen", but it is hidden in Info and I only opened it at 45 minutes. Praise with a fix: put a short hint in the first fight (small to medium).

7. Enemy variety is real. The Nuno sa Punso (shell, ward, haste, chain lightning), the Aghoy (heal, haste, shell, a revive sprout) and Papanoks (haste, fire kick) make each fight different, and the Aghoy's Green Dream rule (kill the other one first or it comes back) is a puzzle I could solve. Praise (small).

8. Defending to re-order the bar is a lovely accidental skill: spending a turn on Defend slides Hina next to Kailo for the Combo. The turn-order queue is the best idea in the game. Praise (small).

9. The tool wheel is empty and the trees are walls. After an hour I own no tool, yet three rooms are blocked by trees. The grove with a shop is the clearest case: a stall in plain view that cannot be reached. Medium. Needs a sign (an NPC, a line) that points at the thing the player lacks.

10. Boar and Papanok fights are too many and too long for the rewards. 20 minutes were boars. Drops are mostly sell items for a lodge that does not exist yet. The Pork is the only thing that mattered (Noelani's list). Medium. A way to skip trivial fights (running past, or fewer boars) would help.

11. HP economy: the only healing is the after-fight feed (+10 to 14) and Noni Pulu (30). Kailo ended most fights at a third health. The game wants me to rest but resting does nothing. Medium to large. A bed or a camp near the north end would fix it.

12. The Simon lights game is fine as a flavor, but it takes 15 to 20 seconds each time and nothing about it changes. Four rounds every time. After 6 Combos it is a chore. Small to medium: shorter after the first win, or scale it.

13. Combo availability is murky. The rule seems to be "both full, adjacent at the front of the bar". A grayed button with no tooltip makes the player think it's a bug. Small.

14. Writing: Hina's in-battle lines ("No shame in living to fight another day. Just don't get used to it.") do more characterization than any cutscene. Praise.

Review so far

Nuitai is trying to be a Chrono Cross style RPG with a turn-order bar that is also a timing game, plus a lot of small food and island life on the side. The strongest parts are the Combo, the parry that deals damage back, Hina's voice, and a Bestiary that finally gives the numbers. It works when it lets me read a pattern, plan three turns ahead, and execute it. It does not work when it hides the rules: why the Combo is gray, why a Kukui is useless in the wrong bag, why trees are walls, why healing is blocked by a burn. The biggest problem is that after four hours across four sessions there is no save point and I have now lost two games to Game Over. The second loss was my own mistake, but a game that kills you for a bag slot and then erases an hour is not ready. I would recommend it to patient players who love combat systems and tolerate loss, not yet to most. If a save point and one sentence about burns appeared tomorrow I would recommend it much more strongly.
