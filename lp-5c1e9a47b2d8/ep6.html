Design notes

1. Praise, large: the parry system is the best action-RPG idea here. Once the narrator gives me times and I press them, almost every normal blow is parried ("Parried!" chime), damage falls from 40 to near zero and the counter comes. It rewards skill without a second control scheme.

2. The turn-order bar with time costs is clear and good: I can see three enemy actions coming and choose Defend to slide. It also makes the Combo rule hard to see, since the actor must be next to the partner.

3. The Game Over costs are inconsistent: two Game Overs in 60 minutes, lost 7 and 16 minutes. The totem saves help but there is only one totem I know of on my usual path, and the next one is a long way from the boss. Medium to large. A boss with no save before it, and no heal station nearby, and Hina stays KO after a win: a bad stack.

4. Beast moves cannot be parried and the only teacher is Hoku, upstairs in the Temple School, which a player skips. The first boar fight should teach "guard first" at the moment of the first big hit. Large.

5. Escape being per character is good fiction (someone covers the retreat) but it can strand the leader alone. It needs a line of text. Small to medium.

6. Equip in battle is free and one wrong page returns to it: an accident that costs 30 HP and a Strength swap is too cheap to trigger. The menu should reset to Attack every turn. Medium.

7. The bed nap fully heals for no clock cost and no money and the ponds heal and cure burns: those are the real heal items, and they are far from the roads. A long road with no pond or bed cannot be done at full strength. Medium.

8. Burn blocks healing and ticks on every action; Hina in nature stance burning at 61 is the same death as in Episode 4. The Kukui is the cure and now drops from boars, but it can only be used from a battle bag. Medium.

9. Praise, medium: stance switching changes the body (Kailo water 210 HP, END 19, Str 14 versus fire 170 HP, Str 17). Choosing a stance for a boss is a real decision. The Nuno's mid-fight shifts then punished the guess; a boss that changes stance should telegraph its next one.

10. Praise, medium: the writing keeps being the best part. Ama's new line, the priest's teaching on the sword in the sand, "Hrrm. I'll be here." from the troll, the chapter title on every save slot.

11. Preemptive punch is a great little system: face a monster, press x, free turns.

12. The Combo's cost is high and its rule hidden; when it works (Pōhuehue 5 of 5, Tinju Surya) it is the best moment of a fight. Medium: explain the adjacency rule on the grayed command.

13. The monsters respawn on every room change: the 8-tile walk from the orchard to the toll is 3 to 5 fights each way. Fighting is the game's content right now, and it repeats. Large for a player who wants to explore.

14. Shop UX: "right adds ten" bought 11 baits when I wanted 1. Small.

Review so far

Nuitai is trying to be a Chrono Cross-style RPG with timing and a lot of tenderness in the writing, set in an island world with a cooking hero. What works: the parry meter, the preemptive punch, the stance switch that changes the whole body, the turn bar, the Combo when it fires, and the writing, which is warm and funny and has real history behind it (the Haka teachings are good). What does not: the roads are all fights, the party loses its one healer to a single Sun Slash, beast moves cannot be parried and the only place that says so is a stairway school, a boss that shifts stance and kills you without a save in reach, hidden rules (Combo adjacency, who escapes, menu memory), and a tool (the digging stick, the rod) the dialogue promises and never hands over. I would still recommend it to patient players who like to learn by dying and who love the writing; not yet to anyone who wants to be guided.
