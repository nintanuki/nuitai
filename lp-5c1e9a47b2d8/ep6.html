After that I want to find out three things: where fish come from for Noelani's stew, whether the old man on the far dock is how we leave this island, and whether the Trapper's ten boars are worth chasing on the way. The island has felt finished for a session, so I am hoping this episode points us at the sea.

Mark Here we are, by the totem. The garden plot by the grocer has sprouts in it now, which is a nice small touch: the seeds we planted last time are visibly growing.

Mark First errand: Melia and the tusk. She is in the west half of the village, by the two big houses, not hiding this time. Let's see if the studio's fix holds.

Mark "A tusk! A real one! From the Great Boar?" Hina cuts in with "We took him down together, you know.", which is exactly her: she will not let Kailo take the credit alone. And Melia: "Mahalo, Kailo! I'm going to wear it on a string. Forever!" The fix works. The handover is automatic, which I like better than picking it from a list; it plays like a scene, not a transaction.

Lelua is not just the name of Kailo's fire stance, it is a martial art, and it comes from somewhere called the Fire Islands. So Kailo's fighting style is borrowed from another place. I'd guess his father brought it home, or learned it out there.

I'll take my sandals off. ...wait we don't wear sandals." That made me smile. It is the first time Kailo has been the joke rather than Hina's target.

Last episode I complained that nobody said anything; one line from the right person is enough.

Mark Plan now. Fish are the one thing on the list I have no lead on, and the only water people I have seen are the old man on the far dock east of the palm maze. So I am heading north, through the forest and the lodge, and I will take any wild boars on the way for the Trapper's count.

Mark Through the forest. The lanu post by the river asks if I want to offer Lanu a cup of water; I said yes, "Kailo poured Lanu a cup of water." Nothing visible came of it. I did it because it is in character, and because a game that asks usually remembers. I also checked the tool wheel here by the river, hoping for a rod or a net: only the Ko'i. Design notes

1. Melia taking the tusk automatically, as a little scene ("Close your eyes and hold out your hands"), works and is warmer than a menu. The fix works. Praise, medium.

2. Keo's "You really beat the Great Boar?" is the right person to notice. One line fixed my complaint that the village ignored the boar. Praise, small.

3. The two Lelua kids explain Kailo's stance name and place it in the world (the Fire Islands) through ordinary villagers. Good, light worldbuilding. Praise, small.

4. The Hunter's Seer's Shell line is exactly the kind of steer I asked for: it names the tool and leaves the use to you. Praise, medium.

5. The relic descriptions ("Bracer. Fire resist, nature weak.") work and make relics a choice. Praise, medium.

6. A shop where A presses go from the greeting straight through to a sale is easy to buy from by accident. A count screen that starts at zero, or a final yes/no, would stop it without clutter. Medium.

7. Selling is one press with no confirm or "Sold" line. Medium, for the same reason as above.

8. The command menu should open on Attack at the start of every fight, not on the page used last. Small.

9. The bounty counter on the win screen, ending with "That's the bounty. Back to the lodge.", is clear and saves trips. Praise, medium.

10. The hidden route: the small dock at the edge of the east wood leads to an islet and then to the long dock I could not reach in Episode 3. Nothing pointed at it, but the area map showed the dock and the island is small enough that edges get walked. That is the right amount of hiding. Praise, large.

11. The old man on the dock was a monster drawn like a person. A sprite that looks like an NPC and fights you is a fair trick once, but it should be a deliberate surprise; here it felt like a misread. Small to medium.

12. Nuno sa Punso was very easy for something posted on the only bridge to new rooms. Small.

13. The boardwalk maze over the lagoon is a good change of texture after many woods. Praise, small.

14. Maika's scene is the best writing this session: a found person, a request not to be found, Hina's "She's worried about you" and his "worried is the least of them". It turns a fetch quest into a decision with a cost on both sides (the shoyu, or keeping his trust). Large praise.

15. Ti Leaf's description, "Ti plant leaf.", does not say what it is for, unlike every other item. Small.

16. Aghoy stance shifts and wind-ups are announced in text ("shifts to light stance", "readies Glamour!"). That gives the stance read without the Shell for a monster that shifts in front of you. Praise, medium.

17. Glamour ("hardens against magic") is a good counter to my fire habit: it asks for plain strokes. The game is starting to answer my routine. Praise, medium.

18. Perfect blocks during an ambush filled Kailo's mana before he moved. The block system now carries whole fights. Praise, medium.

19. A string's leftover hit carrying to the next enemy when one falls mid-string (Hina's Strong, Kailo's Strong for 53) works smoothly. Praise, small.

20. Noelani's list not counting the Coconut and Pork in the bag, with no way I found to hand them over. Medium.

21. "recovers 0 HP" with "+0" over a napping boar is noise. Small.

22. Which enemy blows have a block meter is still unclear (Sting had none). Medium.

23. The game is still easy on this island; the only new challenge was the Glamour shell. The mountain and the moth look like the next step. Small.

Story and characters

The story so far is a village day that keeps widening. The first hours were a stew, a wake and a boar; this session added a second island culture (the Fire Islands and Lelua), a mountain with something huge on it, and a family quarrel (Miki and Maika) that lands in Kailo's hands. Old Pono's wake is still the clock; Kailo promises Keo the story "After the wake", so the wake is still ahead. The tone stays warm and teasing, with a seriousness underneath around family: Kailo's father, Miki's missing brother. Both are people who left.

- Kailo: kind and direct. He gives Melia the tusk like an older brother and tells Maika straight away that his sister is looking for him, before Maika can ask anything. He keeps promises to kids (Keo). The sandals joke shows he can laugh at himself.

- Hina: sharp as ever. She claims her share of the boar ("We took him down together"), shuts down Maika's flirting in one line, and then defends Miki to him. She is the conscience of the pair.

- Melia: delighted with the tusk, "I'm going to wear it on a string. Forever!" A kid getting what she asked for.

- Noelani: busy and brisk, "elbow deep in something". Still runs the stew.

- Keo: wants the story, gets a promise for after the wake.

- The Trapper: unchanged; business.

- Miki: still searching, still offering the shoyu. Now I know more about her than she knows.

- Maika: Miki's brother. Charming, vain, evasive ("A wise man doesn't run away. He relocates."), a medicine seller in a hidden clearing. He says Miki is "a great many things", which hints that their quarrel has two sides. The best new character so far.

- The child on the east bank: a moth "as big as a house" on the mountain, and a mother who does not believe it.

- Nuno sa Punso: an old man of the mound, a spirit monster, not a person.
