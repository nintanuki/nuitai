Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark First session with Nuitai. I know nothing about it beyond the box: a 16-bit style RPG, made by a small studio, and a booklet I have not opened. I want to find out what kind of RPG it wants to be, whether its systems teach themselves, and whether I'd want to keep going after an hour. I'm also watching for bugs, since this is a cartridge build.

Mark Title screen. Sunset over open water, one little island with a palm on the left, the logo painted in sunset colors fading to sea blue. Nice. New Game and Continue, and Continue is greyed out, which makes sense on a fresh start.

Mark There's a line along the bottom, "2026-11-18 save 21". That looks like a build or debug stamp rather than something meant for the player. On a cartridge I'd flag it. Also "save 21" next to a greyed Continue is a little contradictory, if it means there are saves.

Mark New Game. Straight into black cards with white text, typing out.

Mark "In a world covered by endless ocean, people live on scattered islands, venturing across the waves at their own peril." Then sea monsters, storms that "swallow ships", and "miracles and strange wonders are commonplace." Waterworld meets Chrono Trigger's opening crawl. Commonplace miracles is an interesting promise. If miracles are ordinary, the story probably has to be about something that isn't.

Mark "Far to the northeast lies a small, verdant island, untouched by the troubles of the wider world. Here live a warmhearted young cook and a headstrong, devout priestess..."

Mark A cook as the lead. I like that. "Untouched by the troubles of the wider world" is the kind of line that exists so the island can stop being untouched by about hour two.

Mark "Kulāiwi Village. The last day of Summer." Last day of summer is a deliberate choice. Something ends tonight.

Mark And we're in. Two big thatched houses, hens, baskets of produce. That's Kailo, the cook, in the red headband. He's muttering his to-do list: "Stew for the wake tonight... cakes and breadfruit for the next island." A wake. So somebody died, or a wake means something else here. And he's sending food to another island, so there's trade or at least neighborliness across the water.

Mark A note popped along the top, "Lore page added: 'Ulu". So there's a codex, and it fills up from what's mentioned in dialogue. 'Ulu would be the breadfruit.

Mark The portrait art is lovely. Warm, painterly, a lot of personality in his grin.

Mark Melia comes running: "Kailo! KAILO!" She looks younger. Little kid portrait, missing tooth.

Mark "Those naughty boars are back! Three of them, in the taro patch." Kailo: "The taro? That patch feeds three houses!" Good, that gives the stakes a number I can feel. It's not just a nuisance, it's food.

Mark Then Hina walks up. "Then stop shouting and do something about it." And she wanted one of his taro cakes. That's the priestess, I'd guess, from the flower in her hair and the intro card, but the game hasn't said so yet.

Mark Melia wants to fight. Kailo says it's too dangerous. "Fine, at least I get to practice with my sling." "Melia, put that thing away before you break something." "Aww." It's light, a bit sitcom, but quick. I can see the family dynamic in four lines, so it's doing its job. I'd bet that sling comes back.

Mark "Shoo! Shoo!" and a boar squeals, and we're in a battle.

Mark Side view battle, party on the left, enemies on the right, like Final Fantasy. Hina: "You do remember how to fight, right" and "...Really? You lunkhead. Fine, I'll remind you." Tutorial framed as teasing. Fine.

Mark Two boars on screen. Melia said three. Maybe the third shows up later, or it's still in the patch. I'll keep an eye on it.

Mark Each character has two little element icons beside their name. Kailo has a fire one and a water one, Hina has a pink flower-ish one and a yellow star or sun. And seven little marks under each HP bar, all dark. Some sort of meter.

Mark "The faces on the right are who goes next. The top one goes now." A turn order strip, like Final Fantasy X. And "What you pick decides how soon you go again. So think, for once." So actions have weight in time. I like that; it makes every choice a trade.

Mark Command menu: Attack, Cook, Element, Item, with little arrows either side meaning there's another page. The cursor is a fishhook. Cute, and it fits a fishing village.

Mark Attack, pick a boar, and now a list: "1-(88%)", "2-(73%)", "3-(58%)", and a yellow bar beside it. Hina: "Those yellow bars are stamina. Every stroke spends some." Oh, this is Chrono Cross. Light, medium, strong strokes, each with a hit chance, out of a pool of points. I know this system and I like it.

Mark Light stroke, 6 damage. Hina explains the blue marks: "That blue under your HP is mana. Every hit you land builds it." And the next light is now 98%. Hits feed accuracy, hits feed mana. Then "Moves like your Sauté and my Heal spend it."

Mark Another "Lore page added", this one "Lelua". Nobody has said that word yet. A lore page for a word I haven't heard is a strange moment to get one. Not a bug, but the timing reads oddly.

Mark She says "See Strong's number?" and "Now move down to Strong. Watch your face on the right." The menu doesn't say Strong anywhere. It says 1, 2, 3. I moved down one, to 2, and pressed A.

Mark Nothing. Pressed it again. Nothing. B does nothing either. Up and down still move the hook, but no button takes. I tried every button on the pad. For about a minute I thought the game had locked up.

Mark I'm opening the booklet, because I'm stuck. Looking for how attacks work. It says strokes are "LIGHT costs 1, MEDIUM 2 and STRONG 3." So Strong is row 3, and row 2 is Medium. Hina's "move down to Strong" meant two rows down, and the tutorial silently refuses anything else. No sound, no message. Moving to row 3 worked at once.

Mark That's a real problem for a first battle. The menu needs the words Light, Medium, Strong on it, or Hina needs to say "the third one", and a wrong choice during a lesson should get a buzz or a line from her, not dead input. B not working is the worst part. It felt like a crash.

Mark Once I was on row 3, "See that? Your face went down!" The turn strip previews where your next turn lands as you hover. That's a lovely bit of feedback when you can see it working. "Everything tires you. Strong tires you most, so you wait longer."

Mark The strong stroke lands for 41. Big jump over the 6s.

Mark "That's enough mana. Show that pig your Sauté!" Under Element there's Sauté and a second entry, "Lelua", in red. So Lelua is a move, maybe one I can't afford. I go Sauté on Boar A and... "Wild Boar A dodges." The move the tutorial built up to whiffed, and Hina says nothing about it. A scripted demonstration should probably not be able to miss. Or if it can, she should rib him for it, which would suit her.

Mark "Defend is left of your commands. Press left." There's a side page: Equip, Defend, Escape, with Escape greyed out. You can't run from the tutorial. Fair. Hina defends: "Hina braces for the blow." And her own menu has "Scold" where Kailo has "Cook". Her signature move is telling people off. That's character in a menu.

Mark Hina hits like a light: 3, then strong strokes for 21 and 23. Boar A has now eaten something like 180 damage and is still standing. These boars are sturdy for a first fight. Kailo goes 6, then strong 35 and strong 41. Another lore page, "Māla Hula", during Hina's attack. These are coming fast.

Mark Now the boars attack. A bar in the left window with red arrows sliding into a gold zone. "Here they come. Each red bar is one of the pig's hits." "Press A as each bar crosses the yellow. Middle hurts least." "Dead center and you parry it. Not that you'll manage."

Mark Three dead center. "Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird." And Kailo counters on his own, 40 to Boar B. Timed blocking with a reward for perfect timing, like Paper Mario or Legend of Dragoon, folded into an ATB-less turn system. The prompt to page through Hina's box before the meter starts took me a few presses; each page seemed to need an extra A, which I'll keep watching.

Mark Boar A braces, then heals itself for 37. "Lanu's beasts always heal. Nature heals, but it burns." There it is, the first piece of elemental logic, delivered as folk wisdom. Lanu sounds like a god or a spirit of growing things. Nature heals, fire burns nature. Kailo carries fire. So the play is fire on the boars, and I'd bet Sauté is a fire move.

Mark "Wild Boar A shifts to fire stance." Enemies change stance too. Then the boar casts "Sauté" on Hina for 38 and the screen says "Weak!" A boar using a move called Sauté. So Sauté isn't Kailo's personal cooking trick, it's just the name of a fire element anyone can learn. That's a little funny, a pig sautéing a priestess, but it also tells me elements are shared tools, the way Chrono Cross does it.

Mark "Aduh. Fine, I'll Heal myself. Watch how it's done." And "Word learned: Aduh". So there's a dictionary too, separate from the lore pages. That's a nice touch for a setting that clearly borrows from Pacific languages.

Mark Her element list has a header, "Māla Hula", with her two stance icons, then Heal. So Lelua and Māla Hula aren't moves, they're stance names. Kailo's fire stance is Lelua. That explains the lore pages.

Mark I picked Heal and pressed A twice without reading the pointer. It was on Kailo, who was at full health. My fault, but she had just said she'd heal herself, so I'd expect the cursor to start on whoever is hurt. Then the boar burns her: 41, "Weak!", "Burn". I try to heal her properly and get "Hina is burning and cannot be healed." So that's what "Nature heals, but it burns" meant. Burn shuts off healing. That's a sharp rule and I like it, but Heal stayed selectable on a burning target, and I lost the turn finding out.

Mark "I'm hurt, you're fine. Switch to Wai'Kenpō. Water guards." "You won't hit as hard, but you can take more hits."

Mark On the stance header, A pops up a window over the name to confirm the switch. The window is empty. A blank white-bordered box, no text, no moves under it. The switch still works when I confirm: "Kailo shifts into Wai'Kenpō," and his HP goes from 130 max to 160 max. "The yellow box by your name is on water now. That's your stance."

Mark So stances trade damage for health, and change your weaknesses. Water stance raises max HP. I'm curious whether the current HP scales with it or just the cap.

Mark Block meter again. First blow perfect, then I was late on the second and it got through. The boar's three blows land on Hina. "Hina falls." In the tutorial. She got burned, couldn't heal, and that was that. I didn't play it well, but this is a steep first fight.

Mark I checked Item: "Noni x3", no description at all. It won't target Hina while she's down, so it can't revive. Cook lists "Poi", no description either, and hovering it pushes my next turn way down the strip, behind five boar turns. I looked Cook up in the booklet, curious what it does: it makes one meal that feeds the whole party, served on his next turn, ruined if he's hit before then. Good to know. Not worth it solo with Hina down.

Mark Back to fire stance to go after Boar B. In Wai'Kenpō the element list is empty, so water has no moves yet. Switching back, his HP becomes 68 of 130 from 84 of 160. So current HP scales with the max. That answers that.

Mark Boar A drops to a light and a strong. And the narrator, the tool I have running, keeps saying "Wild Boar A: standing" after the screen says "Wild Boar A falls." The screen is right; it's gone. The tool is confused, not the game.

Mark When A died mid-turn, the stroke list stayed up showing "1", "2", "3" with no percentages, and the next stroke just went to Boar B on its own. Fine, but it'd be clearer to snap the pointer to the next target.

Mark Sauté on B in nature stance: 26, "Weak!", "Burn". And now the boar can't heal. That's the lesson Hina's line was setting up, and I learned it twice, once from each side. It's well built when I lay it out like that.

Mark Its blows on me in fire stance come back "Resisted.", 7 damage. Fire stance resists nature, water stance is weak to it. Fire beats nature, nature beats water, water beats fire, presumably.

Mark Odd bit: after one turn of a light, a strong and a light, the stamina bar shows one point left when by the booklet's costs I'd have two. I'm not certain I counted right, so I'll watch it.

Mark "Wild Boar B curls up." Then "Sleep", "Wild Boar B is out of breath!", and the burn ticks it down. A strong stroke finishes it. "Kailo's Perception up!" Stats grow by use, like SaGa or Final Fantasy II.

Mark "Element slot unlocked", a row 1 with two boxes. It doesn't say whose slot. I'll check the menu later.

Hina: "It's not all ruined. Some of those roots are only bruised." That's a kind line from her. She's been needling him all fight, and here she's the one finding the bright side.

Mark Prediction: the Great Boar is real, and it's not just greedy, something has driven it or changed it. Melia's version is a kid's story, but games don't put a boss at the top of the woods for nothing, and "why do they keep coming?" is a fair question nobody answered.

Design notes

1. The stroke system (light, medium, strong with climbing hit chances, out of 7 points) is clear and satisfying once understood. Praise, large.

2. The turn strip that previews where your next turn lands as you hover a choice is excellent feedback. Praise, medium.

3. Timed blocking with a counter for three perfect parries is the best part of combat: fair window, real reward. Praise, large.

4. The tutorial names "Strong" but the menu only shows numbers, and the wrong choice is silently refused. Put Light, Medium, Strong on the menu or have Hina say "the third one", and give a buzz or a line on a wrong press. Large, it's the first five minutes.

5. Stances are a strong idea: enemies flip stance to hit your weaknesses, burn shuts off healing, and nature/fire/water read as a triangle. But enemy stance is never shown on screen, so the player can't plan around it. Show a stance icon on enemies. Large.

6. The Taong Tuod pair is a difficulty spike: poison, blind, sleep and healing on two enemies, in the first forest, placed where you walk into them. Hina's escape hint saved it. Consider one healer instead of two, or move them off the path. Medium.

7. Saving and reviving at totems, with "Tell the Akua what happened", explains game systems in the world's own terms. Praise, medium.

8. Post-fight "Kailo feeds everyone" healing suits a cook hero and cuts down on menu healing. Praise, medium.

9. Lore and word banners pop too often, up to five at once in conversations. Collect them and show one note at the end of a talk. Medium.

10. Item and Cook menus in battle need on-screen descriptions; "hold Select" is taught by a side NPC, not in the battle. Medium.

11. Per-character Escape is interesting but surprising; one line in the tutorial or a confirmation would fix it. Small.

12. Small enemy behaviors (truffles, curling up, sulking from Scold) make enemies feel like animals. Praise, small.

13. The fetch quest list is long (18 items) and some sources aren't obvious yet (pork never dropped). Being able to hand in partial progress is good. Medium.

14. Tool messages that teach the rule at the moment ("The roots are too thick. Sand digs easier.") are well done. Praise, small.

15. Tutori, the tutorial kid who knows he's a tutorial, keeps fourth wall jokes contained to one character. Praise, small.

Review so far

Nuitai wants to be a 16-bit RPG in the Chrono Cross mold, set on a Pacific island, with a cook for a hero. The combat is the strongest part: the stroke chains, the turn strip preview, timed parries with a counter, and stances that change your weaknesses all fit together, and once they click it's a thinking person's battle system. The world is written with care. The loremaster's few lines did more world-building than most opening hours, and the quiet grief around Kailo's house lands without anyone explaining it.

What doesn't work yet is teaching and readability. The tutorial silently refuses the "wrong" stroke, enemy stances are invisible, battle items have no descriptions, and the first real forest encounter is a two-healer status wall placed where you walk into it. There's also a layer of small rough edges: clipped text, name mismatches in lore banners, an empty stance window, numbers that change without a message.

Would I recommend it today? To people who love Chrono Cross and Paper Mario style timing, yes, with a warning that the first hour has a few spots where you'll think the game broke. For a general RPG player, not yet: fix the tutorial refusal, show enemy stances, and soften the tree fight, and I'd recommend it more widely.
