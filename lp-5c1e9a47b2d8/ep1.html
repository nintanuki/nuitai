Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark This is the first session. I know nothing about this game except the name on the box, Nuitai, and that it is a 16-bit style RPG. No previous sessions, no reviews, and I have not opened the booklet. What I want to find out today: what kind of RPG this is, how the battles work, whether the game teaches itself, and who these people are.

Mark Title screen. A sunset over the sea, one small island with a palm tree, gentle light on the water. The logo has an ocean gradient in the letters, warm on top, blue underneath. That already tells me the setting is islands and water. Continue is greyed out, which is right for a fresh save. New Game.

Mark The opening is a series of black text cards. "In a world covered by endless ocean, people live on scattered islands, venturing across the waves at their own peril." Then sea monsters, storms that swallow ships, "miracles and strange wonders are commonplace." It is a classic opening crawl, short enough that it does not overstay. The last card names our pair before we meet them: "a warmhearted young cook and a headstrong, devout priestess." So I expect to play the cook.

Mark "Kulāiwi Village. The last day of Summer." Last day of summer is a nice ominous stamp. Things are about to change.

Mark And here is the cook. His name is Kailo, red bandana, standing over three baskets of food. "Stew for the wake tonight... cakes and breadfruit for the next island." A wake. Somebody has died, and the game drops that in casually, in the middle of a chore list. I am filing that away.

Mark One small thing already: his first line breaks mid-sentence across three pages, "for the next" then "island." The box fits about three short lines, and the writing is not cut to fit it, so sentences get split in odd places.

Mark Melia runs in shouting his name. Big grin, missing tooth in the portrait, so a kid. "Those naughty boars are back! Three of them, in the taro patch." Kailo: "The taro? That patch feeds three houses!" Good line, it tells me the stakes in village terms, not world terms.

Mark Then Hina. Red flower in her hair, very dry: "Then stop shouting and do something about it." And "I was coming over for one of your taro cakes." I like her immediately. She is the priestess from the opening card, I would guess, the "headstrong" one, though nobody has said so yet.

Mark Melia wants to fight, gets told to watch the stew, and settles for "at least I get to practice with my sling." Kailo tells her to put it away. Prediction: Melia's sling comes back, either she saves somebody with it or she follows them somewhere she was told not to go. A sling that gets mentioned in scene one is going to be fired in act one.

Mark Cut straight to the taro patch, no walking. Three boars in the crops, a fruit stall on the left, a man in a feather cloak with a lei by the big house. "SQUEEEE!" The game is moving fast; I have not had control of Kailo yet.

Mark Straight into a fight, and Hina teaches it. I missed a line of Kailo's here because it went by on the cut, but her reply tells me what it was: "...Really? You lunkhead. Fine, I'll remind you." So he forgot how to fight and she is reminding him. That is an old trick for a tutorial and it fits her.

Mark First thing she points at is the column of faces on the left. "Those faces on the left are who goes next. The top one goes now." Then: "What you pick decides how soon you go again. So think, for once." This is a turn order you can see ahead of time, like Final Fantasy X. Three boars on the map, two in the fight, which I notice but do not mind.

Mark Commands are Attack, Cook, Element, Item. The cursor is a fish hook. That is a lovely touch.

Mark Attack opens a second list: Light, Medium, Strong, each with a hit chance, and a yellow bar beside it. "Those yellow bars are stamina. Every stroke spends some." So a turn is a string of strokes until the stamina runs out. That is Chrono Cross, almost exactly, and I like Chrono Cross's system, so I am already comfortable.

Mark Light hits for 6. Mana goes from 0 to 1, and Hina explains: "That blue under your HP is mana. Every hit you land builds it." The second Light lands and the Strong chance climbs: "Each hit you land makes the next likelier." Good, the numbers on the menu move right in front of you, so the rule is shown, not just told.

Mark This is the best moment of the tutorial so far. I move the cursor down to Strong and my own face in the turn column slides down two places before I have committed. "See that? Your face went down!" You see the cost of a choice before you make it. That is exactly the right way to teach it.

Mark Strong lands for 39. Light did 6. That gap is huge; Strong is six times a Light for more stamina and a later turn. I will be watching whether Light ever earns its place beyond raising the hit chance.

Mark "That's enough mana. Show that pig your Flame Kick!" The Element list has a header, "Lelua", with a fire icon and a water icon next to it, and the move is spelled "FlmKick". The abbreviation is a little ugly; there is room on the line for the full name. I do not know what Lelua is. A stance or a school, maybe, since Hina's says "Māla Hula" with a flower and a sun.

Mark Flame Kick: 24 damage, "Weak!", and "Wild Boar A catches fire." Then, on the boar's turn: "Wild Boar A is burning and cannot be healed," and "Wild Boar A moves and the burn flares: 12." So burn punishes acting, and it stops healing. The boar tried a Mud Wallow and the burn shut it down. That is a neat interlock and the text explains it as it happens. One thing that bothers me: Flame Kick at 24 is weaker than a plain Strong at 39. The element is "Weak!" and still does less. The burn makes up for some of it, but on raw numbers the special move looks worse than the basic one.

Mark Hina has her own command where Kailo has Cook: "Scold". I tried it because it is too good a name not to. It is a mash: "Scold!", thirteen presses wanted in three seconds. I was slow and only got the end of it. "Hina scolds Wild Boar B." "Wild Boar B starts sulking." Sulking skipped the boar's next three turns. That is strong.

Mark And then the price. Hina's mana went to minus five and her bar turned red. Scold borrows mana you do not have, and until she pays it back, Scold and Heal are both greyed out. The game never told me Scold cost anything, and the menu has no cost column. I found out by seeing red. I like the idea of a move that puts you in debt; I do not like learning it after the fact.

Mark Cook did nothing when I pressed it. No sound I could hear, no message, the cursor just sat on it. I assume it needs something I do not have yet, but the game should say so.

Mark The second boar hit Kailo with "Tusk Charge" for 24. Then a Light, Medium, Strong string from Kailo, 6, 21, 41, and Hina finished it with a 4. Victory.

Mark The results screen is just stats and money. No experience, no levels mentioned. VIT, STR, INT, END, WIL, AGI, PER, LUK. 24 by a cowrie shell and 0 by a gold coin, so two currencies, shells and coins. "Received" is empty. No drops from boars.

Mark After the fight: "Kailo feeds everyone." and "Eat up. Nobody fights on an empty stomach." HP ticks back up on the results page. The cook heals the party after every battle. That is the character's job turned into a system, and it means I can spend HP more freely between fights. I like it a lot.

Mark Back on the map, the scene keeps going. Half the taro is dug up, Hina says some roots are only bruised, and Kailo: "...yeah, bruised taro still cooks. I just hate seeing the rest go to waste." That is a good character line, a cook who hates waste.

Mark Then Melia, quietly: the boar that ran off went through the stew, "The pot's on its side, and it's all over the sand." Kailo: "...the wake's tonight." The ellipsis does a lot of work. Melia tells us about "The Great Boar" at the top of the woods who sends the little ones to steal taro "because he's too fat to come get it himself." Hina: "That is not how boars work." Ha. I said no jokes, but that line is the game's, and it is well timed.

Mark Prediction: the Great Boar is real, and Hina is wrong about how boars work this time. Kid legends in RPGs are always true. I will also guess that something is driving the boars down out of the woods, not just hunger, and that it ties into whatever the opening card meant by "strange wonders."

Mark Kailo cancels the trip to "the next island" and decides to go up and "tell him to get his own taro." Hina will not come yet: "When you figure out you can't do this alone, you know where I live." And: "Be back before dark. Old Pono's wake is tonight." So the wake is for Old Pono. Prediction: Old Pono's death matters to the plot. A wake on the first day of a game is never just a wake.

Mark Kailo to Melia: "Boars don't care how brave you are." Then to himself: "I should head home first, maybe Noelani can help." I do not know who Noelani is. Family, I would guess, because he says home.

Mark Control at last, about seven minutes in. The village is small: a fruit stall with a grey-haired man behind it, a man in a feather cloak with a lei, someone in brown by the big house, the beach and sea along the bottom. Let me talk to everyone before I go anywhere.

Mark The person by the big house is just "Villager": "They say the Callers in Makua talk to the dead. Gives me the shivers." Two new names in one line, Callers and Makua. Prediction: we go to Makua, and someone wants a Caller to reach Old Pono, or someone else who has died.

Mark The man in the feather cloak is not a man, it is a totem. Kailo explains it: "Every totem on the island listens. Tell the Akua what happened, and they remember it for you. And they get anyone who's knocked out back on their feet." So totems are save points that revive the party, and it is said in the world's own terms. That is how I like save points explained.

Mark The save file is named "The Pork Strikes Back." Chapter titles on the save slot. That one made me smile. Only Kailo's face on the file, so Hina really has gone home.

Mark The grocer opens with "E komo mai! What'll it be?" Noni Pulu, a poultice for 30 HP, and then a list of food and seeds: taro, wheat, breadfruit, pineapple, pia, taro seed, wheat seed, 'uala seed, akai seed. Seeds mean farming, probably. Taro heals 5. I bought two Taro on a guess that Cook in battle wants ingredients, since pressing it with an empty bag did nothing.

Mark Keo, a kid on the beach, wants to come fight the Great Boar. "I'll be really quiet." Kailo: "'A'ole. Not this time, Keo, it's too dangerous. But I'll tell you everything when I'm back, I promise." Two kids in two scenes asking to come along and both told no. That is twice now. I am more confident Melia or Keo will follow him.

Mark Kailo's house. The banner says "KAILO's House" with his name in capitals and the rest not, which looks like a template.

Mark Noelani is here and already knows: "I heard what happened. The boars, the pot, all of it. I'll help you cook it. Don't make that face." Then Kailo recites the recipe: "4 Fish, 3 Egg, 3 Coconut, 2 Pork, 2 'Uala, 2 Akai Lau, 'Awapuhi and Māla Shoyu." That is a fetch quest with eight ingredients, and Noelani will keep track: asking "How many ingredients do I have?" gets "Still missing" and the list. That is a good quest log in a person. Kailo calls Noelani "ma'am" and Noelani tells him not to track mud in and offers to hold his "pule," which I take to be his money, so Noelani runs this house. Kailo's "I'll take my sandals off. ...wait we don't wear sandals." is a cute beat.

Mark Prediction: 2 Pork comes from the boars, and the Great Boar is where the pork for the wake ends up. The cook's revenge on the pig that spilled his stew. That would be very on theme.

Mark "My dad's chest." Inside: Eku, Ko'i and Kukui. None of those words mean anything to me yet, and the pickup text does not say. I had to open the bag to learn that Eku is an oar used as a weapon, "Raises Vitality but lowers Agility," Ko'i is a "Stone adze. For chopping wood. It was his dad's.", and Kukui is "Candlenut oil. In a fight, cures a burn." Past tense, "It was his dad's." So his father is dead or gone. Prediction: Kailo's father was lost at sea, and that loss will come back as a plot thread. The adze went straight onto the Y button, so it is a field tool, Zelda style. I expect trees in the way somewhere soon.

Mark A small complaint. "Found Eku." tells a new player nothing. One line, "Found Eku, his dad's old oar," would teach the word and the item at once. The game is using Hawaiian words generously, which I like for the setting, but it needs to gloss them the first time.

Mark Back outside, I faced a hen and pressed A: "Kailo found an Egg." Pressing again: "The hen clucks. No egg yet." So eggs are a renewable pickup. There are three hens around the two houses and the recipe wants three eggs. I got all three in a minute. That is a nice little teach: the first ingredient is right outside the door.

Mark The figure up in the corner was Melia hiding. "You found me!" Kailo: "Hide and seek is over. Go finish your chores." And then, the second time: "Do you think your dad's ever coming back?" Kailo just says "..." and she says "Sorry..." and then, brightly, "Go get the Great Boar, big brother! Bring me back a tusk!"

Mark So the father is not dead, he is gone, and nobody knows if he is coming back. "Your dad," not "our dad," so I do not think Melia is his real sister; "big brother" sounds like something she calls him. That revises my guess: Kailo's father left, by sea most likely, and did not come back. I still think it becomes a plot thread. I will go further: I think he is out there, alive, and we find him or what happened to him before the end.

Mark I tried the adze on a palm by the edge of the village. "Sound: a dull bump," nothing else. Either village palms cannot be cut, or I need a different kind of tree. No hint either way.

Mark North out of the village: "Kulāiwi Forest." A river bends across the top, a chest on the far bank, and enemies walking on the map, a boar and a rooster. Visible enemies, no random encounters. Good, I get to choose my fights. I walked into the boar.

Mark Two boars and Kailo alone. Before swinging, I tried Cook again, now with two Taro in the bag. Still nothing, no message. This is the third time. I am stuck on what Cook even is, so I opened the booklet and looked up just that word. It says Cook picks "a meal you have cooked at home," then you stir with the D-pad, and the party eats on Kailo's next turn. So Cook is empty because I have never cooked anything in the pot at home. That makes sense, but the battle menu could have told me. A greyed-out Cook, or a line like "Nothing cooked yet," would have saved three wasted turns. While I was there, my eye caught the line about the adze above it, ten chops to fell a tree. I will keep that in mind but I did not go looking for it.

Mark The fight itself: Light, Medium, Strong does about 67 to a boar. Then they get a long run of turns, and a meter I have not seen before slides up in the left window: red arrows moving toward a gold zone. Nobody explained it. I pressed A as the arrows hit the gold, and it said "PERFECT" and "DODGE." Three blows, three perfects, no damage, and a point of mana for my trouble. That is the Super Mario RPG timed defend, and it feels good. But I learned it because the arrows plainly want something; Hina's tutorial never mentioned it.

Mark Two things that did hurt. The boar uses "Truffle!" to heal itself 26, then 30, which undid most of a combo. And "Tusk Charge" did 51 to Kailo with no block meter at all. The first Tusk Charge in the village did 24, so either Hina's Defend was covering something or this one rolled high. I cannot block it and I cannot see it coming, so it is the one thing that actually threatens me.

Mark Kailo's Perception went up after the fight, with a little green arrow. No experience points, stats go up through use, like SaGa. Drops: Noni Pulu, "Seed of Strength", "Seed of Perception". No pork. My pork prediction is not looking good yet; maybe pork comes from somewhere else.

Mark The after-fight meal healed only 13 this time. In the first fight it healed more while I waited. I suspect I cut it short by pressing confirm. If so, the game is quietly charging me HP for being impatient.

Mark Next, the rooster on the map turns out to be two "Papanok." Big white roosters with red tail plumes. Same opening: Light, Medium, Strong.

Mark This fight taught me the most so far. The second Papanok used "Preen!" to heal 12, then "shifts to fire stance" and hit me with "Burning Wings" for 18. So enemies have stances too, and they change them. I went into Kailo's Element menu and found that the "Lelua" header is a switch: "Kailo shifts into Wai'Kenpō." The moves change from FlmKick to "WaveGrd", and his max HP jumps from 130 to 160. His hits got a little weaker, 5, 17, 32 instead of 6, 21, 41. So fire is the attacking stance and water is the sturdy one. That is Lelua and Wai'Kenpō: two stances, two element colors. Now I understand the two little icons next to each name on the party panel.

Mark The rooster answered by moving to "nature stance" and then healed itself three times in a row: Preen, "Nest Song" for regeneration, Preen again. It went from 20 percent back to 70. That was the puzzle: a healer that will out-heal my combos. I remembered the first fight, "Wild Boar A is burning and cannot be healed." So I switched back to Lelua, which costs a turn, and opened with Flame Kick. "Weak!", "Papanok B catches fire." One combo later it was dead and it never healed again.

Mark That was a proper little puzzle, and the answer was something the tutorial showed me by accident in the first fight. I want to say clearly: this is the best design in the game so far. Stances, burn, and healing enemies fit together, and I solved it from what the screen had told me.

Mark Two complaints. The window that offers the stance switch flashes over the stance name and was blank in my screenshot, so I was not sure what I was confirming until the text said "shifts into." And switching costs the whole turn, which is fair, but the turn-order preview does not show that cost before I commit the way it does for Strong.

Mark Drops: Noni Pulu and an Egg. I waited on the after-fight page this time to test my theory, and the meal still healed exactly 13. So I was wrong earlier: it is a fixed amount, not something I cut short by pressing confirm.

Mark North part of the forest: a chest with "'Ōlena" in it, again no gloss, and the river lets me scoop water: "Kailo scoops up some fresh water." Fresh water for cooking, I assume, since the stew needs a pot of something.

Mark Then a big house in the clearing, sign: "Hunters' Lodge."

Mark The lodge is full. A Trapper runs a shop and a bounty board. I made a mistake here: I pressed through the menu too quickly and my shells went from 56 to 26. The buy list opened on "Drain," an element at 30, and I think I bought it without seeing a confirm. It does not show in the sell list or the bag tabs I know, so I cannot be sure where it went. A purchase should always ask "Buy this?" or at least show a "Bought Drain" message. Losing half my money without knowing what I got is the worst kind of mistake for a player, because I cannot tell whether to blame myself or the game.

Mark The Trapper's stock is all elements and weapons at 30 and 40: Drain, "Kapa Wraps", "Kagura Fan", "Hamaya", "Ihe", "Hula Ki'i", "Tokotoko", "Ananá Bracer", "Ananá Sash", "Ananá Lei". Kagura and Hamaya are Japanese, which is a surprise next to all the Hawaiian. A trading post that brings in things from far away, maybe.

Mark The bounty: "Bounty: the Great Boar. Pays 500 pule." So pule is money, and Melia's Great Boar is real. That half of my first prediction is right: the trapper confirmed it before I ever saw it. Hina said "That is not how boars work," and she may still be right about the why. I took the bounty: "Good. Come back when it's done."

Mark Miki. "Have you seen a man with white hair and a mouth that never stops? Tall. Looks like he's doing you a favor by existing." She is his sister, he "walked out of Māla Iki without a word," and their mother wants him home. She has Māla Shoyu, the last ingredient on Kailo's list, and will trade it for her brother's whereabouts. That is a neat way to tie a fetch item to a character. Prediction: the white-haired brother joins the party. A character introduced by a description that rude is always a party member, and probably a smug mage.

Mark Two Hunters give tips. "A Seer's Shell tells you what a beast is made of, and how much fight it has left." "A relic guards you from one element and leaves you open to another." And the useful one: "Everyone who fights carries three kinds of weapon, and each kind has its own Skill... In a fight, Weapon is left of your commands, next to Defend." So I can swap to the oar in battle and Cook becomes something else. Good. NPCs as a tutorial you opt into.

Mark In the corner sits Gajumaru, a turtle in a robe, who speaks... very... slowly. "Do you... know... Kaigara...?" "Shells... and... patience..." Kaigara turns out to be Go on a nine by nine board, with cowrie shells for stones. I placed one stone in the middle to see how it plays, he answered on the diagonal, and then I resigned, because I came here for a boar, not a Go match. "You resigned. Gajumaru wins." Then: "The shells... were kind... to me... today..." I love that this is here. Go is a strange and brave thing to put in an RPG. It also gives no rules at all, so a player who does not know Go is lost; I only knew what to do because I already play.

Mark East of the lodge: a wider forest screen with two boars and two walking tree stumps on the map, a path north, and a wooden dock on the sea to the east. A dock means a boat, and Kailo was going "to the next island" this morning, so that is how we leave Kulāiwi later.

Mark Before going north to whatever lives "at the top of the woods," I turned around. Hina told me plainly, "When you figure out you can't do this alone, you know where I live," and the boar fights have shown me Kailo alone takes a lot of damage he cannot block. The game told me where help is, so I am going to take it. It is a three-screen walk back, which costs a couple of minutes, and the walk itself is quick.

Mark "HINA's House," same capitals as Kailo's banner. She is by the shelves, and Kailo notices: "Wait, are you packing?" "For my trip. I leave soon, and it's none of your business." Then: "Did you forget what you were doing? You're hopeless, aren't you? Well, you need a healer anyway, so I'm coming too." "Hina joined the party. While she is in your party, the fallen get back up after a win."

Mark That is a good scene. She saves his pride by pretending he forgot, which is kind, under the scolding. Prediction: Hina's trip is the real start of the story. She is "devout," so it is a pilgrimage or a calling, probably to Makua and the Callers who "talk to the dead," and Kailo goes with her once the boar business is done. I also think her trip and Old Pono's death are connected; maybe Pono was the old priest and she is going to replace him.

Mark Back east with Hina, and a boar catches us on the path. This time Hina has a lesson ready, over the block meter: "Here they come. Each red bar is one of the pig's hits." "Press A as each bar crosses the yellow. That halves it." "Dead center and it misses you. Not that you'll manage." That is the explanation I wanted three fights ago. It only plays once Hina is in the party, so a player who goes up the hill alone, as I did, meets the meter cold. And her box sits over the meter. I pressed through her last page and straight into the meter, and my first two blocks were wasted. Two misses, Hina took 31.

Mark Hina's Heal: "You're bleeding on the taro. Hold still, I'll Heal you." Ha. 18 HP for a point of mana. Modest.

Mark I tried the Hunter's tip. Weapon is at the left with Defend and Escape, and the oar is the only other thing in the list. "Kailo takes up the Eku." His max HP went from 130 to 160, Vitality as promised, and Cook became "Paddle". Paddle is "Mash A!" for a couple of seconds, every press a hit. It took the boar to half. That is a strong Skill. It also cost all my mana and one more, so Kailo went to minus one.

Mark Then the boar slept, used "Mud Wallow", "Truffle" and "Nap", and healed back to full. Hina said the thing I had figured out on my own: "Lanu's beasts always heal. Nature heals, but it burns." So the game does say it, through her, just after I needed it. Lanu sounds like a name, a god or a spirit of nature. Good line, and it confirms the rule I found with the rooster.

Mark Two plain combos and the boar fell, and it dropped "Pork." So pork does come from the boars, it just is not every time. One of the two pork for the stew.

Mark I found what that 30 shells bought. The bag has grown two tabs, Elements and Seeds, and under Elements is "Counter x1", "Guards allies and hits back," with a red and blue icon, Kailo's two colors. So I did buy an element at the lodge without meaning to. The buy confirm problem stands, but at least I know what I own now. I used three Noni Pulu, two on Kailo and one on Hina, before going further.

Mark North of the east forest is a little maze of palms with green fairies walking around in it, then a crossroads, then "Lanu's Grove": a square pond, people praying, a totem. I saved. Inside the game the file says 0:17 played.

Mark The grove fills in the religion. An old villager: "My knees hurt. I pray anyway. Lanu grew the knees, she can listen to them complain." Another: "I bring Lanu water from the spring every morning. It is hers anyway, but she likes being asked." The Sage: Lanu is "The Akua of the sky and the forests... She is our mother, the mother of all living things." And "The temple is for Haka. The grove was here first." So there are at least two Akua, Lanu for growing things and Haka with a temple, and some tension between the old grove and the newer temple. These lines are the best writing in the game so far. Each villager gets one sentence and it tells you who they are.

Mark Prediction: Hina is a priestess of Haka, not Lanu, and that grove-versus-temple line is going to matter to her. The Akua talk to people through totems; I expect that is literal later, an Akua actually speaks.

Mark The grove is a dead end, though. The area map, which only shows rooms I have visited, made it clear I had skipped the side roads, so I went back to the crossroads. East was a dead end with a walking stump, "Taong Tuod", that ambushed us: "Ambush!" and "Stump Slam" on Hina for 33. Flame Kick was "Weak!" on it too. West of the crossroads is a wider wood with an exit north. A pair of Papanok on the way, and Kailo's Paddle took one from full to twenty percent in one mash. Two eggs from them, which gives me more than the recipe needs.

Mark And here it is. A clearing with bare patches of dug-up earth, six small red boars standing around, and in the middle a huge black boar with a spiked mane. "A huge boar with a mane." Melia was right, it is the Great Boar, and the little ones really do gather around him. The dug patches are a nice touch: this is where the taro went.

Mark Hina, as we get close: "That's him. Are you ready?" and a clean choice: "Fight the Great Boar now?" with "Fight now" and "Go back." I like a boss that asks first. We are at full health and saved two screens back.

Mark The battle name is "Tandayag na Opon," which I take to be his real name. In battle he is barely bigger than the regular boar beside him, which undersells him after that huge sprite on the map.

Mark I opened with Hina's Scold on the boss, on the theory that three skipped boss turns is worth a lot of mana. "Tandayag na Opon does not listen." Fair, bosses are immune, but it still cost her all seven mana and put her at minus seven. Then the little boar's Tusk Charge did 70 to Hina. Seventy. The same move has done 17, 24, 41, 45, 51 and 70 to us. I cannot find the pattern, and it is the biggest threat in the fight.

Mark Flame Kick on the boss did 11 and no "Weak!" He is not nature. Then he used "Squeal!": "Wild Boar B comes running!" And the important one: "Wild Boar B uses Truffle on Tandayag na Opon! Tandayag na Opon recovers 109 HP." So the little boars are his healers, and he keeps calling more. This is the puzzle: you cannot outdamage a boss that has a healer for every one you kill.

Mark Then "Tandayag na Opon winds up for Flame Tusk!" That told me two things. He is fire, which is why Flame Kick was resisted, and the wind-up gives one turn to answer. I switched Kailo to Wai'Kenpō, and the Flame Tusk landed for 37, "Resisted." In water stance with the oar, Kailo's max HP is 200. The warning-then-answer is very well done; the game told me the element right when I needed it.

Mark So the plan now is: Kailo stays in water, the minions die first, and Hina keeps us alive. I have killed two of them, and he has called a fourth, "Wild Boar D," which immediately hit Hina for 51.

Mark Hina has a running commentary in this fight that helps. When Kailo dropped to 31: "You look awful, and I can't Heal yet. Eat a Noni, lunkhead." That is a hint in her voice, and a good one, though "can't Heal yet" was odd since she had seven mana; I think she meant it was not her turn.

Design notes

1. The turn order preview that moves while you hover a choice is excellent. Hovering Strong slid Kailo's face down the column before committing. The cost of a choice is visible before you pay it. Praise, large.

2. Stances, burn and healing enemies fit together as a puzzle. A healer that out-heals your damage, and burn that stops healing, taught by the first fight's "cannot be healed" line. I solved it from what the screen showed. Praise, large.

3. The boss fight is out of balance with what the game has taught by then. The boss stayed near full HP for fifteen minutes. Summoned boars heal him (109 in one Truffle) and hit for up to 70. A boss with two elements that punish both of Kailo's stances, against a party whose only heal is 18, felt unwinnable without a tool I had not been shown. If Cook, Wave Guard or a Seer's Shell is the key, the game needs to point at it before the clearing. Large.

4. Skills and elements need their cost and effect on the menu. Scold, Paddle and Wave Guard all had effects or costs I learned only by using them. The bag and shop have description lines; battle menus should too. Medium.

5. Cook should be greyed out or say why it does nothing when there is no cooked meal. Three silent presses is a dead button. Medium.

6. The block meter is a good timed defend, in the Super Mario RPG mould, and perfect blocks feel great. But it is explained only by Hina, only once she joins, and her text box covers it. Explain it the first time it appears, and keep the lesson box off the meter. Medium.

7. Shop buys need a confirm, or at least a "Bought X" line. I lost half my money without knowing what I got. Medium.

8. The cook feeding the party after every fight ("Kailo feeds everyone.") turns his job into a system. It healed a fixed 13 or so each time, not more if I waited. Praise, small.

9. Totems as save points that "remember it for you" and revive the fallen are well explained in-world. The game over cost me only the fight and put me back at the last totem. Praise, small.

10. Hina's tutorial voice works. "So think, for once." "Not that you'll manage." It teaches and builds her character at the same time. Praise, medium.

11. NPC tip-givers at the lodge (weapons and Skills, relics, the Seer's Shell) are an opt-in tutorial. Good, but the Weapon tip should come before the player needs it. Small.

12. Hawaiian and other Pacific words give the world a strong identity. The first time each appears, the pickup line should gloss it ("Found Eku, his father's oar"). Small.

13. Visible enemies on the map and a "Fight now / Go back" prompt before the boss are both player-friendly. Praise, small.

14. The Great Boar's battle sprite is barely bigger than a normal boar, after a huge sprite on the map. Small.

15. Enemy names mix languages: Papanok, Taong Tuod, Tandayag na Opon. It suggests different islands with different peoples, which could be great, but right now nothing in the game frames it. Small.

16. Weapon swaps change max HP (130 to 160 with the oar, 200 to 210 in water stance) and the Skill. That is a rich system, shown only by a Hunter's tip. Small.

17. Kaigara, a full Go game with a turtle in a lodge, is charming and brave. It needs a rules page. Small.

18. Dialogue is not cut to fit the three-line box, so sentences break across pages mid-phrase. Small.

Story and characters

The story so far is small and warm, and I like it. It is the last day of summer on a quiet island. A cook has to remake the stew for Old Pono's wake because a boar spilled it, and the boars come from a Great Boar at the top of the woods. Underneath that are bigger threads the game is careful not to push: Kailo's father is gone and nobody knows if he is coming back; Hina is packing for a trip she will not talk about; the Callers in Makua "talk to the dead"; there are Akua, Lanu of growing things and Haka with a temple, and the grove "was here first." The tone moves between comedy and grief easily, and the writing is best in one-line NPCs.

- *Kailo.* The cook, kind and a little scattered. He hates waste ("bruised taro still cooks"), keeps promises to kids, and goes quiet when his father comes up. His job is his combat style, which I like.

- *Hina.* The priestess, and the best-written character so far. She scolds everyone, calls Kailo "lunkhead," and is kind underneath it. She pretends he forgot what he was doing so he does not have to ask for help. The game calls her "she."

- *Melia.* A child, eager to fight, with a sling. She calls Kailo "big brother," but says "your dad," so she is probably not his sister. She asks the question nobody else will, about his father, and apologizes for it.

- *Keo.* A child who wants to come and promises to be quiet. Kailo promises to tell him everything.

- *Noelani.* Runs Kailo's house; Kailo calls Noelani "ma'am." Sharp and practical, holds the recipe list and the money jar. The game has not said exactly who Noelani is to Kailo.

- *Miki.* From Māla Iki, looking for her brother, a white-haired man "with a mouth that never stops." Proud, holds the shoyu as a bargaining chip. The game calls her his sister.

- *Gajumaru.* A slow-speaking turtle who plays Kaigara. Gentle and odd, and I want to know where he came from.

- *The Sage and the grove villagers.* One line each, all good. They make Lanu feel like a real presence.

- *Tandayag na Opon, the Great Boar.* Real, guarded by small boars that heal him, fire and thorn. He has not said a word.
