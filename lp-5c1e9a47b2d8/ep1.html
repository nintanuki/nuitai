Design notes

1. The turn-order queue with real time costs is the best idea in the battle system. Heavier moves send you further back, and Sauté sends you hardly at all. Reason: it turns a menu into a tempo puzzle and rewards reading the faces on the right. Praise. The cost: nothing teaches that elements are fast, only that "Strong tires you most." Medium for the missing piece.

2. The Light, Medium, Strong combo with an accuracy chain is good, and Hina's line "Each hit you land makes the next likelier" is the whole key. A Strong first blow dodged again and again, while Light then Medium then Strong landed 65 to 72 each time. Reason: it teaches risk over several fights. Small, but the tutorial doesn't tell you the order to use; it should show the odds when you pick (the Select hold wasn't visible to me). Medium.

3. The tutorial voiced by Hina as an insulting teacher is excellent. "...Really? You lunkhead. Fine, I'll remind you." Reason: it teaches without a wall of text and builds her character in the same lines. Praise.

4. Tutorial text is slow and each page needs a press. The text typed out letter by letter, each "z" first finished the line, and the parry lesson had four pages I had to step through with the meter already on screen. Medium. Fast-forward on the lesson pages would help.

5. The parry meter is a good, readable idea that fits "magic is motion," and the mana reward for parrying (+1 per parry) makes defense feel active. Praise. But the timing information given as "seconds to the middle of the zone" is hard to use because each press costs about 0.17 seconds and neighbors shift. With three close blows it is impossible one at a time. Medium to large for anyone not using a narrator, because the zone is small.

6. The parry meter does not appear for every enemy attack. Hits on the second party member and some big hits (the 43 from Papanok) arrive without one. Reason: it breaks the rule I had just learned and leaves Hina out of the defense system entirely. Large for fairness, since Hina went from 38 to KO while I was busy parrying for Kailo.

7. Hina's KO state is a blocker. She stayed at 0/160 through three fights and a nap, and the game's text said fallen allies get up after a win. Reason: the promise on the join message is not kept, and I found no reviving item. Large.

8. Cooking as a delayed action that hits can ruin is a strong, original risk-reward idea. "Kailo's Poi is ruined!" is the moment I understood it. But it spends the whole mana bar before it even finishes, and the first time I used it there was no warning. The second time I started it by accident, it served for +16 HP, which is weaker than a free Noni Pulu. Medium. It needs a hint line in the Cook menu and a better payoff than 16 HP.

9. The Stir and Mash minigames are well-explained by the narrator and fit the setting. The Stir feels good when it works, and "steady matters more than fast" is a good rule. Praise. Small.

10. The enemy with a self-heal and no visible health bar wasted ten minutes of the hour. The forest boar healed +37 twice per round and my 70-damage combos did nothing. Reason: I couldn't see that I was losing, and the answer (cheap fire Sauté on its nature stance) was never suggested. Large.

11. The stance-and-weakness system works and is satisfying to discover. "Weak!" and "Resisted" pop-ups on every hit teach it by feel, and the enemy "shifts to nature stance" log line is the only cue. Praise for the idea. Medium for the delivery: stance should be visible on the enemy while fighting.

12. The status screen's per-stat growth ("EXP to next: Vitality 93/100," "Each point adds ten HP") is a strong design choice. Reason: it ties who Kailo becomes to what I do, and shows my next increase. Praise.

13. The equipment compare screen is clear: arrows in yellow and red next to each stat when the cursor is on the new gear. Praise. Small.

14. The Info menu (Quests, Bestiary, Glossary, Lore, Records) is rich and arrives with dozens of details, but I only found it after fifty minutes. Reason: nothing points to it, and the quest log and bestiary would have answered several of my early questions. Medium. A first-time prompt would solve it.

15. Quests are given in a way I like: Noelani's list lives in her mouth ("Still missing: 4 Fish, 2 Egg..."), but the same list in the quest log would help a lot. Small.

16. Writing and world are the strongest part. The Loremaster's three answers each carry a theme (religious schism, syncretic belief, magic as motion) and the gods' names change by island. Praise.

17. Visible enemies on the map, with the "!" alert, give a fair warning, but fights start suddenly at close range and I lost control a few times while walking. Small. Also, no way to see monster positions without the narrator list.

18. Art and sound are excellent for the setting: the title screen, the Chief's Hut, the forest, the faces in dialogue. The animation is readable. Praise.

19. Gating by tools (no tool yet on Y, "you're bad at gardening," "I need a cup of water") is honest and visible. Small. The water offering was solved by pressing z on a pond, which the post's text didn't suggest.

20. The loose items the game leaves around (taro, eggs from hens later) support the cooking theme and show a systems-in-world approach. Praise. Small: the hen said "No egg yet" and there was no hint when to return.

21. The opening fight is too long and too punishing for a first fight. Two boars took 15 minutes, nearly killed Hina, and gave me five systems at once. Reason: stamina, combos, mana, Scold, parry, weak and the queue all arrived inside one fight. Large for pacing.

Story and characters

Kailo is a cook, warm, a little proud, and the type to treat a ruined stew as a personal insult. He follows his stomach and his heart: he decides to go and "tell" the Great Boar "to get his own taro" because the stew was for a wake. His line "...yeah, bruised taro still cooks. I just hate seeing the rest go to waste." and "I'll take my sandals off. ...wait we don't wear sandals." are my favorite things he says. His father's chest holds an oar from the Kame Kingdom, so someone in that family traveled and fought.

Hina is the sharp one: "You lunkhead," "You're hopeless, aren't you?" She hides care behind insults and insists she is packing for a trip on her own business, then comes anyway because Kailo needs "a healer." She is Kailo's partner in the tutorial, and the one who explains the rules in her own voice. She is probably the "devout priestess" from the opening card. Her stances are nature and light, and she is weak to fire.

Melia is the younger one: loud, shouts "Kailo! KAILO!", wants to fight with her sling, told to stay and guard the stew from the gulls. She feels like Kailo's younger sister or close neighbor, and she loses the stew to a boar and says sorry. Noelani runs Kailo's house and may be family: kind, practical, "Don't make that face." Chief Ulu is calm and invites Kailo to bring the stew to the wake. The Loremaster (I don't know their gender) is wise and playful, and is the source of the world's religious history. Nene, who lives by Pono's house, misses Pono and wants poi. Old Pono is dead, never seen, but described by three people through small details (the ginger, the stew, the poi). The Grocer is a shopkeeper only.

The tone is warm and domestic with a deep layer of religion and history under it. The themes I see forming: food as love and mourning, the hundred-year-old split between the old Akua (Lanu) and the newer Shaka faith, magic as movement, and an island on the edge of a larger world with Kota Emas, the Kame Kingdom, Makua and Velafi mentioned already.

Review so far

Nuitai is trying to be a warm, hand-built 16-bit island RPG in the Chrono Cross mold, a place where cooking, farming, gods and fighting are all the same thing: motion. After an hour I think it is a good deal more than a homage. The writing is the strongest thing: small, specific lines (the ginger, the taro, "we don't wear sandals"), a living cast, and a Loremaster who explains a century-old religious split in four answers. The systems have a real idea at the center: your actions have time costs, fire beats nature, parrying is how you fill your mana, and cooking is a delayed move you have to protect. When it works, as in the seven-for-seven parry and the Burn on the healing boar, I'm really into it.

What doesn't work yet is the way the game teaches and tells. The first fight crams everything into a fifteen-minute slog. Hina is knocked out for most of my second half and nothing I could find brings her back, in spite of the join message. The enemy HP and stance aren't on screen. The parry is hard to time by numbers when blows are close. The Info menu, the best help in the game, is hidden. There are also a number of non-interactive objects and a few dead ends.

Would I recommend it today? Yes, to people who like Chrono Cross or Final Fantasy X and don't mind a slightly rough first hour. The world and the battle system are worth the time, and I would want the Hina problem and the opening-fight pacing fixed before telling a friend to start. I'd give it another hour in a heartbeat.
