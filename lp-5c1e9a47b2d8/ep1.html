Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark Hi, I'm Mark. This is my first session with Nuitai, a 16-bit style RPG I've been asked to play completely blind. I know nothing about it beyond the name. There's no previous episode, so nothing to recap. What I want to find out today is what the game is about, how its combat works and whether it teaches itself, and whether the first hour gives me a reason to keep going.

Mark The title screen is a sunset over the ocean, a small island with a palm tree on the left, the sun's reflection breaking up on the water. The logo is in warm sunset colors fading into ocean blue. That's a confident first image: this is an ocean game, probably islands. "Continue" is greyed out, which makes sense for a fresh save. Music just started, called "Main Theme". New Game it is.

Mark The intro is text cards on black, typed out. "In a world covered by endless ocean, people live on scattered islands, venturing across the waves at their own peril." Then "Sea monsters lurk beneath the surface, unpredictable storms swallow ships, and miracles and strange wonders are commonplace." and "It is a world as beautiful as it is deadly..."

Mark Fairly standard framing, but it does its job in three cards and doesn't overstay. Then the cast: "Far to the northeast lies a small, verdant island, untouched by the troubles of the wider world. Here live a warmhearted young cook and a headstrong, devout priestess..." A cook as a lead is unusual. I'd guess the cook is who I play and the priestess is the partner.

Mark "Kulāiwi Village. The last day of Summer." Setting a story on the last day of a season is a promise that something ends today. My first prediction: by the end of this opening, the island's peace is broken and the two of them leave it. "Untouched by the troubles of the wider world" is the line that tells me so.

Mark And we're in. I'm Kailo, the cook, with a red headscarf and a warm, slightly cheeky portrait. First line: "Stew for the wake tonight... cakes and breadfruit for the next island. If I leave now I'll be back before Melia's done her chores." So there's a wake tonight, meaning someone died, and I'm cooking for it. That's a heavier note than the sunny village suggests. Melia must be the priestess. The baskets in front of me (water, greens, something red) read as a kitchen set outside a hut, which fits a cook.

Mark Small thing on presentation: the dialogue breaks mid-sentence across boxes ("for the next / island."). It's readable, but a line-break-aware pager would feel more polished.

Mark Melia runs in shouting "Kailo! KAILO!" Her portrait is bright and gap-toothed, all energy. "Those naughty boars are back! Three of them, in the taro patch. They're digging up everything!" Kailo's answer tells me what he cares about: "The taro? That patch feeds three houses!" He thinks about food as who it feeds, not as ingredients. Good, quick characterization.

Mark Then Hina steps out of the hut on the right with a red flower in her hair: "Then stop shouting and do something about it." Then "I was coming over for one of your taro cakes. I'd like there to still be taro tomorrow." Dry, direct, a little bossy. So I was wrong a minute ago: Hina, not Melia, is the most likely "headstrong, devout priestess". Melia gets told to "stay here and keep the gulls off the stew", pushes back with "Let me fight with you", and Kailo says no. She wants to "practice with my sling", and he says "Melia, put that thing away before you break something." She reads as a kid or a younger sibling who wants in on the action.

Mark Prediction: Melia's sling comes back. Either she disobeys and shows up in this fight, or she joins the party later and the sling is her weapon. Setting up a forbidden weapon in scene one is a Chekhov's gun.

Mark Hard cut to the taro patch, and there are the three boars, rooting among the plants. Kailo shouts "Shoo! Shoo!" There's a market stall top left, an older man in a feathered headdress near the big hut, and a few villagers watching. No transition, but it reads clearly.

Mark A boar answers "SQUEEEE!" and we cut straight into battle with "Preemptive strike!" along the top. Battle music changes to a track called "Spiny Shell". The party is Kailo and Hina, so Hina is coming with me. Kailo has 130 HP, Hina 160, and each has two small element icons by their name: a flame and a wave for Kailo, a pink flower and a yellow sun-like burst for Hina. Under each HP bar there are seven small empty marks. I don't know what those are yet; my guess is a charge or turn meter.

Mark One thing I noticed: there were three boars in the patch, and Melia said three, but only two are in this fight. Maybe the third is a second wave.

Mark Hina asks "You do remember how to fight, right?" with No above Yes, and No highlighted by default. That's a tutorial gate, and putting No first is a kind default for new players. I genuinely don't know this system, so I'll say No and let it teach me.

Mark Hina: "...Really? You lunkhead. Fine, I'll remind you." I like that. The tutorial is in her voice, and the teasing tells me these two have known each other a long time.

Mark Once the intro box clears, the background is a beach with turquoise water and a rock arch, much nicer than the flat yellow I saw during the transition. On the right is a column of portraits: "The faces on the right are who goes next. The top one goes now." Then "What you pick decides how soon you go again. So think, for once." That's a turn-order timeline, like Final Fantasy X's. The order right now is Kailo, Hina, Kailo, Hina, Kailo, then both boars, then Hina. We got three turns each before they move, which is the preemptive strike paying off.

Mark The command menu is Attack, COOK, Element, Item, with little arrows left and right, so there may be more pages. The cursor is a fishhook, which is a nice touch. COOK in capitals is clearly Kailo's personal command. Hina says "Start with Attack. Even you can manage that." Doing what I'm told.

Mark Attack opens a second menu: "1 Light 88%", "2 Medium 73%", "3 Strong 58%", then Element, with a tall yellow bar beside it. Hina: "Those yellow bars are stamina. Every stroke spends some." This is Chrono Cross's combo system almost exactly: you chain light, medium and strong hits inside one turn, each costs stamina, and the percentages are hit chances.

Mark Light hit lands for 6. Two things happen at once and both are visible: one of the seven marks under my HP turns blue, and every hit chance goes up ten points, Light to 98%, Strong to 68%. Hina names both: "That blue under your HP is mana. Every hit you land builds it." and "Moves like your Sauté and my Heal spend it. Don't waste it." So Sauté is one of Kailo's moves, and Hina is the healer, which fits a priestess. Second Light hit, another 6, and "See Strong's number? Each hit you land makes the next likelier."

Mark This is the best part of the tutorial so far. When I move the cursor down to Medium and then Strong, the timeline on the right re-sorts live: Kailo's next face slides down the column. Hina: "See that? Your face went down!" So a heavier stroke delays my next turn, and the game shows the cost before I commit. That is exactly the kind of feedback a turn-order system needs. Showing, then naming, then letting me try is good teaching.

Mark Strong lands for 39, more than six times a Light hit, and three blue marks fill at once, so mana rewards the risky swing. Strong is now greyed out because I'm down to two stamina, and Hina adds "Defending builds a little mana too, not as much as attack chains." Small layout issue: the menu now reads "Medium100%" with no space once the number hits three digits.

Mark "That's enough mana. Show that pig your Sauté!" Element opens a short list: "Lelua" with a little swirl icon, and "Sauté" with a flame. There's no cost, no description, no target info on this screen. I'm only picking Sauté because I was told to.

Mark "Kailo uses Sauté on Wild Boar A!" and "Wild Boar A takes 13 damage." Two surprises. First, 13 is a third of what my Strong hit did, so either boars resist fire or elements are about utility, not raw damage. Second, and this looks like a bug: the fire burst plays on the lower boar, but the 13 pops up over the upper one, which is the boar I've been hitting as A all fight. The effect and the number are on different enemies.

Mark Sauté only took one mana mark, five down to four. And Hina's command list has "Scold" where Kailo has COOK. A priestess whose special move is scolding. That's funny without being a joke, and it matches how she's been talking to Kailo the whole fight.

Mark "Defend is left of your commands. Press left." Pressing left flips to a second page: Equip, Defend, and a greyed-out Escape. And hovering Defend moves Hina's next face up, three Hina turns in a row. So defending is fast, attacking big is slow, and you can see both before choosing. "Hina braces for the blow."

Mark With the tutorial loosened up, I play Hina myself: two Lights for 4 and 3, Strong for 22, Medium for 12. Her hits are noticeably weaker than Kailo's, which fits a healer. Her mana fills to all seven marks. Then back to Kailo; I peek at COOK and the only entry is "Poi", with no description, and hovering it pushes Kailo's next turn way down behind three boar turns. I'll save that for when I know what it does. Light 6, Light 5, then Strong whiffs with a "Dodge" popup, and a Medium connects for 21.

Mark I've now put over 130 damage into Wild Boar A and it's still standing. There's no enemy HP bar anywhere, so I can't tell if I'm close. For a first fight that's a little long.

Mark "Wild Boar A uses Truffle!" "Wild Boar A digs up a truffle." "Wild Boar A is out of breath!" A boar's special move is rooting for truffles, which is charming and on theme. I don't know if it healed or buffed anything though; nothing told me.

Mark Then the boars attack and a block meter appears in my menu window: red arrows sliding left toward a gold zone. Hina walks me through it over three boxes: "Here they come. Each red bar is one of the pig's hits." "Press A as each bar crosses the yellow. Middle hurts least." "Dead center and you parry it. Not that you'll manage." The game pauses the arrows while she talks, which is the right call.

Mark Three blows, three perfect parries, zero damage. Hina: "Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird." That's a great line. It's grudging praise that tells me exactly how she feels about Kailo without saying it. One thing I couldn't tell: which of us the boar was attacking. Nothing on screen pointed at a target.

Mark Then the boar takes a few free swings on its own. "Hina dodges Wild Boar B." But the "Dodge" word floats over a boar, not over Hina, which is the wrong body. Then "Wild Boar B uses Thorn Strike on Kailo!" "Kailo takes 7 damage. Resisted." No block meter for that one. My read is that Thorn Strike is an element, and elements can't be blocked, only resisted. That's a reasonable rule, but the game didn't tell me; I'm inferring it.

Mark Hina: "You're bleeding on the taro. Hold still, I'll Heal you." Her Element list has a header, "Māla Hula", with her two element icons, and one move, "Heal". It restores exactly the 7 I lost. Then she finishes Boar A with a Strong for 25: "Wild Boar A falls."

Mark And now I learn something about the earlier bug. When Boar A falls, it's the lower boar that disappears. So A was the bottom one all along. The Sauté burst was on the right boar, and it's the damage numbers for A that were floating over the top boar the whole time. Either way, numbers and hits don't line up on screen when two enemies are present.

Mark With one boar left, the Element menu now shows a "1" by Sauté, which I take to be its mana cost, and Kailo's header reads "Lelua". Selecting the header offers a switch to "Wai'Kenpō", his water stance. So these headers are stances: each character has two, one per element icon, and switching costs the turn. But the move list under Wai'Kenpō is empty, so there's nothing to gain right now, and I back out. The text in the stance window also blinks on and off, so in some frames it's just an empty box.

Mark The rest is a grind: my Light-Light-Strong-Medium chain does about 75 a turn, Hina's about 40. Thorn Strike hits Hina twice for 9 each, both "Resisted", and B digs another truffle with no visible effect. Hina's Medium for 12 ends it: "Wild Boar B falls."

Mark Victory music is "Cloud Racer". The results screen is a stat sheet: VIT, WIL, STR, AGI, INT, PER, END, LUK. Kailo is the STR 20 bruiser, Hina the INT 20 caster with more HP. There's no XP and no level counter, and a "Received" box that's empty. Money along the bottom: 120 next to a cowrie shell, 0 next to a gold coin. Two currencies with no labels; I'd guess shells are local island money and coins are foreign. No XP is interesting. Prediction: stats grow from use or from food, not from levels. A cook as the hero makes me think meals are how you get stronger.

Mark Overall the first fight was about eight minutes, and it was long for two boars, about 150 to 190 HP each with no visible HP bar. The tutorial itself is very good. The combo system and the live timeline preview are the standouts.

Mark After the fight there's a second page: "Kailo feeds everyone." and Kailo says "Eat up. Nobody fights on an empty stomach." HP ticks up as they eat. That's the cook fantasy built into the victory screen, and it means minor chip damage is free to recover. Smart way to keep a two-person party moving without potions. Hina's bar stopped at 158 of 160 though, so it's not a guaranteed full heal.

Mark Then "Element slot unlocked", with a "1" and two little boxes, one grey and one gold. It doesn't say whose slot or what a slot does. In Chrono Cross terms, I'd guess this is a new place to equip an element. I'll check the menu.

Some of those roots are only bruised." And he answers "...yeah, bruised taro still cooks. I just hate seeing the rest go to waste." That's a nice exchange. She's sharp in a fight and kind once it's over.

Mark Then Melia: "Kailo... I'm sorry. The one that ran off. It went right through the stew on its way to the forest." So that's where the third boar went. Good, the game accounted for it. And the stew for the wake is ruined. Kailo, quietly: "...the wake's tonight."

Mark Melia: "Everybody knows. The Great Boar lives at the top of the woods, and he's the boss of all the boars. So she leaves the party, and the game has told me where to find her when I need her. Kailo to Melia: "Boars don't care how brave you are." And his last line, "I should head home first, maybe Noelani can help."

Design notes

1. Praise: the live turn-order preview. When the cursor moves to Medium, Strong, Defend or an element, the timeline re-sorts to show the cost before you commit. This makes the speed trade-off teach itself. Large.

2. Praise: the battle tutorial is in Hina's voice, paced one rule at a time, and pauses the block meter while she talks. It is funny and clear. Large.

3. Praise: the Light/Medium/Strong chain with rising hit chances and mana from landed hits (Chrono Cross style) is readable and rewarding from the first turn. Medium.

4. Praise: the boars' Truffle and Heal moves make them a real puzzle. Enemies that support each other are a good early lesson. Medium. But the tutorial shows Truffle without showing what it does, so the first solo fight feels unfair until you learn it. Medium.

5. Praise: the game makes "you can't do this alone" felt rather than forced. Fighting solo is a losing battle of attrition, Hina's line points you to her, and the next fight is quick. Medium.

6. Praise: "Kailo feeds everyone" after each win, with HP ticking up, is the cook fantasy built into the loop and keeps a two-person party moving without potions. Medium.

7. Praise: stats grow by use, shown mid-fight ("Kailo's Perception up!") and on the Status page as bars. Weapon types level too ("Tools mastery Lv 2!"). Medium.

8. Praise: stances are a real choice. Lelua is the damage stance, Wai'Kenpō the tank stance with more HP and fire resistance. Medium. But the game never says that stances change stats or which moves live in which stance. Medium.

9. Minigames start with the timer running and no pause on first appearance (Stir, Alternate). On first use each one should hold until the player has read its instruction. Medium.

10. Burn blocking all healing is a strong rule that was explained only by losing my healer to it. A greyed-out Heal should say why. Large: it decided two fights.

11. Fire enemies (Papanok) hit Hina for 35 to 51 "Weak!" each, and three of them can kill her in one round. With burn blocking heals, the second forest area's rooster pack is the hardest fight of the hour, harder than it looks. Medium.

12. No enemy HP bars and no damage-type feedback on enemies ("Resisted" shows for the party only). Fights against healers are hard to read. Medium.

13. The first fight runs about eight minutes for two boars with about 190 HP each. Long for a tutorial. Small.

14. Praise: the stew fetch quest and the 6000-pule soy sauce give a clear, concrete goal; Noelani tracking the list in dialogue is friendly. Small. Eighteen items is a big ask for an opening, though. Small.

15. Praise: diegetic saving (telling the Akua at a totem) and a free nap in your own bed. Small.

16. The personal pouch versus shared bag split is not explained, and it made the Seer's Shell I bought unusable in battle. Medium.

17. Praise: Hawaiian words ('a'ole, 'ae, Akua) are used naturally, with a Glossary in the menu. Small.

Story and characters

The story so far is small and warm: a cook has to remake the stew for Old Pono's wake after boars wreck his taro and spill the pot. Underneath it there is grief (a wake, "this island takes people too young", "my dad's chest") and a hint that the island's peace is about to end ("the last day of Summer", "untouched by the troubles of the wider world"). Lanu, a goddess whose beasts are raiding the village, is the first sign of the bigger world.

- Kailo: a warm, practical cook who thinks of food as who it feeds ("That patch feeds three houses!"). He protects the kids around him and seems to be raising them since losing his father. Stubborn about doing things himself. Easy to like.

- Hina: the "headstrong, devout priestess". Sharp, bossy, very funny ("Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird."), and clearly fond of Kailo. She is about to leave on a "trip" she won't explain. The best-written character so far.

- Melia: an energetic kid who wants to fight, carries a sling, plays hide and seek, and tells the Great Boar legend. Fun, a little pushy.

- Noelani: runs Kailo's house, offers to cook with him, and teases him ("Don't track mud in"). Her relation to Kailo isn't stated yet.

- Keo: a boy who wants to come and fight the Great Boar. Kailo promises to tell him everything.

- Villagers: the Callers in Makua who "talk to the dead"; a kicking teacher; a grocer with no lines.
