Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark Hi, I'm Mark. This is the first session with Nuitai, a 16-bit style RPG from a small studio, and I'm going in blind. Nothing before this, no manual read, no trailers. What I want to find out today is simple: what kind of game this is, how it teaches its systems, whether the combat has an idea of its own, and whether the opening gives me a reason to keep playing.

Mark Title screen. A sunset over the sea, a single palm island on the horizon, the logo in warm-to-cool gradient letters. Ocean and islands, then. Continue is greyed out, which is right for a fresh install. New Game.

Mark Text cards on black, typed out. "In a world covered by endless ocean, people live on scattered islands, venturing across the waves at their own peril." Then sea monsters, storms that "swallow ships", and "miracles and strange wonders are commonplace." Then: "Far to the northeast lies a small, verdant island, untouched by the troubles of the wider world. Here live a warmhearted young cook and a headstrong, devout priestess..."

Mark "Untouched by the troubles of the wider world" is a promise the opening is going to break. That's a classic setup, the quiet village before the fall. A cook as a lead is a nice change from a farm boy with a sword, though.

Mark "Kulāiwi Village. The last day of Summer." The last day of anything in an RPG opening means something ends tonight.

Mark Here's the village. Two big thatched houses, a ring of palms, chickens, baskets of produce. The art is clean and warm. Our hero is Kailo, red bandana, and his portrait has a lot of personality. His first line: "Stew for the wake tonight... cakes and breadfruit for the next island. If I leave now I'll be back before Melia's done her chores."

Mark A wake. So someone has died, and the village is already in mourning before anything happens. And "the next island" means he's about to travel. That's two hooks in one line, which is efficient writing.

Mark Melia comes running: "Kailo! KAILO!" and then "Those naughty boars are back! Three of them, in the taro patch. They're digging up everything!" Kailo: "The taro? That patch feeds three houses!" Good, the stakes are local and concrete.

Mark And here's Hina, in the doorway on the right. "Then stop shouting and do something about it." Dry. Then "I was coming over for one of your taro cakes. I'd like there to still be taro tomorrow." I like her already. She's the priestess from the card, I'd guess, and she's clearly not shy.

Mark Melia wants to fight, Kailo says it's too dangerous, she says "Fine, at least I get to practice with my sling." Kailo tells her to put it away. Prediction: Melia's sling is going to matter, either she joins the party later or she saves Kailo with it at some point in this opening. A game doesn't give a kid a named weapon and a "let me fight" line for nothing.

Mark I was pressing through text a little fast and we're suddenly in a battle. A boar went "SQUEEEE!" and the screen cut over. Hina asked "You do remember how to fight, right?" with No and Yes, and my confirm landed on No, which was the cursor's default. Honestly that's what I'd pick anyway on a first play.

Mark "...Really? You lunkhead. Fine, I'll remind you." Ha. That's a nice way to make the tutorial optional and in character. The battle screen is side view, Kailo and Hina on the left, two boars on the right. Melia said three boars, and I only see two. Maybe the third is coming. Each character has two little element icons next to their name: Kailo has fire and water, Hina has a pink flower and a yellow star. And a row of seven small marks under each HP bar.

Mark Hina runs the tutorial herself, in character, which is the right call. "The faces on the right are who goes next. The top one goes now." "What you pick decides how soon you go again. So think, for once." So it's a turn order you can see and push around, like Final Fantasy X's timeline.

Mark Commands: Attack, Cook, Element, Item, and arrows on the sides that hint at more pages. Cook as a battle command for a cook. I like that a lot already.

Mark Attack opens three strengths with hit chances: Light, Medium, Strong. "Those yellow bars are stamina. Every stroke spends some." So within one turn you string Light, Medium and Strong blows from a stamina pool. That's Chrono Cross, more or less, and Hina even says "Each hit you land makes the next likelier." My Light did 5, the chances on the menu went up after each hit.

Mark "Moves like your Flame Kick and my Heal spend it. Don't waste it." I think "it" is the row of blue marks under the HP bar, which filled one per hit. Either a line about those marks scrolled past while I was pressing, or the game skipped explaining them and called them "it". I'll keep an eye on it. A few lines later she calls it mana, so the blue marks are mana.

Mark Then the nice bit. "Now move down to Strong. Watch your face on the right." I put the cursor on Strong and my portrait in the turn order dropped down the queue before I'd even confirmed. "See that? Your face went down!" That's the whole system explained in one look. The cost of a heavy swing is shown while you're thinking about it, not after.

Mark Strong landed for 42. Big difference from the 5s and 6s of the light blows. "Everything tires you. Strong tires you most, so you wait longer." "But land it and it builds more mana."

Mark Small thing on the menu: "2 Medium100%" with no space between the word and the number. The longer word crowds the percentage.

Mark "That's enough mana. Show that pig your Flame Kick!" The Element list has two lines: a greyed "Lelua" with fire and water icons beside it, and "FlmKick". Hina calls it Flame Kick, the menu squeezes it to FlmKick. I'm guessing Lelua is the name of the pair of elements Kailo is using, a stance or a mode, and it's grey because it's a header, not an option.

Mark Flame Kick did 11. The Strong blow did 42. So either boars resist fire or the elements are about utility and not damage, at least at this level. I'll test that before I conclude anything. It cost one mana mark.

Mark Hina's turn. "Defend is left of your commands. Press left." Left shows Equip, Defend, Escape, with Escape greyed. Fair, you don't get to run from a tutorial. "Hina braces for the blow." Her element page shows "Māla Hula" over "Heal", so the header pattern repeats. My guess about headers holds up so far.

Mark I gave Hina a full combo, Light, Strong, then Lights until the stamina ran dry. Her Strong did 23, her Lights 3 and 4. She's clearly the softer hitter. Then Kailo's turn: Light for 6, Strong for 37, and "Wild Boar A falls." Two Lights on Boar B and then the boar attacks.

Mark The block meter. A bar in the bottom-left window with a gold zone, and red arrows sliding in. Hina pauses the arrows to explain: "Here they come. Each red bar is one of the pig's hits." "Press A as each bar crosses the yellow. Middle hurts least." "Dead center and you parry it. Not that you'll manage." Good on the game for freezing the arrows while she talks. It's a Mario RPG style timed defense.

Mark Three blows, three perfect blocks. "Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird." I'm warming to her. She teases, but she's paying attention, and the writing gives her a little crack in the armor right away. I'm not sure who the boar was hitting, though: the meter doesn't show a face or a name, and neither of them lost HP.

Mark Next exchange, a "Dodge" pops up over the boar and the log says "Kailo dodges Wild Boar B." I read that as the boar missing Kailo, but the word floated over the boar's head, not Kailo's. Later the same thing happens on Hina's misses, and once after I parried all three of the boar's blows on Hina, the log said "Hina dodges Wild Boar B" and the Dodge floated over the boar again. So "Dodge" is used both for a miss and a parry, and the number pops in the wrong place. I had to work out who did what from context.

Mark Then the boar eats something. "Wild Boar B uses Truffle!" "Wild Boar B recovers 30 HP." Hina: "Lanu's beasts always heal. Nature heals, but it burns." That's a hint dressed as a proverb: these animals belong to a nature element, and fire beats nature. Which makes my Flame Kick doing 11 look odd. Maybe the boar hadn't shown its element yet.

Mark And then: "Wild Boar B shifts to fire stance." Enemies change element mid-fight. That changes everything. "Wild Boar B uses Cinder Gore on Kailo." "Kailo takes 25 damage." No block meter for that one, so I think element attacks skip the timing game. Hina: "You're bleeding on the taro. Hold still, I'll Heal you." Heal for 18, and then a second Cinder Gore took 27 right back. Heal is a top-up, not a fix.

Mark So the boar is fire now. Kailo carries a fire and a water icon. If water beats fire, I want water. I go back into the Element list and try the "Lelua" line I thought was a header. It isn't. Confirming it swaps the page to "Wai'Kenpō" with "WaveGrd" under it, and a second confirm spends Kailo's turn: "Kailo shifts into Wai'Kenpō."

Mark That's the stance system, and I found it by poking, not because anyone told me. Hina's tutorial covered Strong, mana, Defend and blocking, and then the enemy changed stance and the tutorial went quiet. The interesting part: Kailo's HP went from 96 of 130 to 118 of 160. The water stance raised his max HP and scaled his current HP with it. So stances aren't only about attack type, they reshape the character. And the block window was wider in water stance, 0.31 seconds against 0.25 before. Defensive stance, defensive numbers. I like that a lot, it's a system with a clear identity.

Mark Prediction: each character has two stances, one aggressive and one defensive, and the fights later on will ask you to switch at the right moment, the way the boar did. I'd bet bosses telegraph a stance change a turn before it happens.

Mark Three more perfect blocks on Hina's behalf. One Strong whiffed, then a Medium for 16. "Wild Boar B falls." Victory music kicks in.

Mark The results screen shows both characters' stats, VIT, STR, INT, WIL, AGI, PER, END, LUK, and what we found: "Pork" and "Wheat". Pork from a boar, sure. Wheat from a boar is a bit odd. 85 of something with a cowrie shell icon and 0 by a gold coin icon, two currencies with no labels. No experience points shown anywhere, so either there's no XP or growth comes from somewhere else. Kailo stays at 160 max HP, so the stance carries out of the fight. And Melia said three boars. We fought two.

Mark After the fight there's a second page: "Kailo feeds everyone." and Kailo says "Eat up. Nobody fights on an empty stomach." HP ticks up while they eat. That's the cook identity carried into the systems, and it's charming. Kailo went from 118 to 134. Not a full heal, so it's not a free reset either.

Mark Back in the taro patch. "Look at this. Half of it's dug up. That's a month of poi, just lying in the mud." Hina: "It's not all ruined. Some of those roots are only bruised." Kailo: "...yeah, bruised taro still cooks. I just hate seeing the rest go to waste." That's a good small character beat. He's practical, but it hurts him.

Mark Then Melia, and the third boar shows up in the story instead of the fight: "Kailo... I'm sorry. The one that ran off. It went right through the stew on its way to the forest. The pot's on its side, and it's all over the sand." So that's where the third boar went. The stew for the wake is gone. "...the wake's tonight."

Mark Melia's folk tale: "The Great Boar lives at the top of the woods, and he's the boss of all the boars. He sends the little ones down to steal taro for him, because he's too fat to come get it himself." Hina: "That is not how boars work." Melia: "Then why do they keep coming?" That last question is better than it sounds. Something is driving them down.

Mark Prediction: the Great Boar is real and is the boss of this opening, at the top of the woods, and the reason the boars are raiding is that something has pushed them out of the forest. My bet is it ties to the "troubles of the wider world" the intro says haven't reached this island yet.

Mark Kailo: "...Change of plans. Nobody's going to the next island today. I have a stew to remake, and if there's a boss boar up there, I'm going to go tell him to get his own taro." Melia: "Aw." Then Kailo thinks: "I should head home first, maybe Noelani can help." A new name. Noelani lives with Kailo, apparently.

Design notes

1. Praise: the turn-order preview moves your portrait as you hover Light, Medium or Strong, so you see the cost of a heavy blow before committing. It's the clearest teaching moment in the session. Large.

2. Praise: stances as named martial arts (Lelua "from the Fire Islands", Wai'Kenpō) that reshape HP and stats give the element system a body and a culture. Large.

3. Praise: enemies that change stance in answer to you, like Boar B going fire after a "Weak!" hit and then tearing into Hina, make each fight a small puzzle. It's the best enemy behaviour in the session. Large.

4. The stance switch needs its own lesson the first time an enemy changes stance. Everything else is taught, and the most important rule isn't. Large.

5. Praise: Hina's proverb "Nature heals, but it burns" is a hint that becomes a rule: fire is "Weak!" on nature boars, and burning stops them healing. Medium.

6. Praise: the perfect-parry counter and the mana it pays make the block meter the strongest tool in a fight, not a side minigame. Medium.

7. Praise: the Loremaster's Mana speech explains why moving builds mana and why burn flares "when the target moves". Fiction and rules agree. Medium.

8. Praise: Scold, a priestess who fights by telling things off, with a real cost ("out of breath"). It's funny and useful. Medium.

9. Rewards and purchases go missing from view: koa wood with no message, the bought element with no pointer to the bag, the Seer's Shell absent in battle. Each one made me think something was broken. Medium.

10. Flame Kick at 11 damage against a 42-damage Strong blow in the first fight teaches the player that elements are weak, until a stance change flips it. A tutorial fight where the boar starts in nature stance would teach the right lesson. Medium.

11. Yes-or-no boxes that start on No for harmless actions (offering water, handing in ingredients) eat presses. Small.

12. Topic menus should move the cursor on or mark what you've heard. Small.

13. Praise: the save totem as the party telling the Akua what happened, and save files named for the chapter ("The Pork Strikes Back"). Small.

14. Praise: Tutori's short fourth-wall joke ("Button?" "Grandpa doesn't get it either.") and the Quests page that writes itself. Small.

15. Chopping takes fifteen presses a tree. As a path gate it's fine; as a resource loop it's slow. Small.

16. The Chief's Loft is an empty room you can walk into by accident on the way to the chest. Small.

Story and characters

The opening is a small, warm village on its last day of summer with grief in every corner: Old Pono's wake tonight, his widow Nene not eating and hearing him hum at night, a father who "left on his adventure", and "This island takes people too young". The boars are a comic problem with a serious edge, and Melia's "Then why do they keep coming?" points at something wrong in the forest. The faith is drawn with care: Lanu, goddess of every green thing, lost the sun to Haka (Shaka), a teacher from the west; some priests walked off and became the Maki Sages; ordinary people pray to both. The stew for the wake ties the whole first act together, with each ingredient a person or a place.

- Kailo: a cook who has fed his household since his dad left. Practical, hurt by waste, and he grieves through food ("Without it, it's eggs floating in coconut. Not for Pono."). Very likable.

- Hina: a Māla Priestess from Kota Emas, leaving on a pilgrimage soon, sharp-tongued and secretly kind ("Fine. That was good. Don't make it weird."). Joins with an insult. The best-written character so far.

- Melia: the kid with the sling who wants to fight and is told to stay back.

- Noelani: runs Kailo's house in all but name, dry and capable ("Don't make that face.", "This house used to be louder.").

- Chief Ulu: warm, proud of Kailo, tells us about Hina's promotion and the dad.

- The Loremaster: the voice of history, with the best line of the session ("Nobody ever told me I had to pick one.").

- Tutori: the tutorial with a joke name, and the joke works.

- Nene: Pono's widow, hungry for Kailo's poi. Small and touching.

- Miki: a sharp, impatient sister hunting her white-haired brother, with the shoyu as leverage and a ready insult for Hina. A strong entrance.

- Keo, the Elder, the Lelua teacher and student, the hunters and the Trapper: they make the village and the lodge feel lived in.
