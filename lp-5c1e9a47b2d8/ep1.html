Mark Plays Nuitai

A blind playtest of the pygame demo by Mark, an invented professional playtester, thinking out loud. His notes follow the episode.

Mark This is the first session. I know nothing about this game except the name on the box and that it calls itself a 16-bit style RPG. I have the booklet next to me and I have not opened it. What I want to find out today is simple: what the game wants me to do in the first hour, how it teaches its systems, and whether the fights have something of their own or are just menus with numbers.

Mark Title screen. Sunset over open water, a little island with one palm, the logo done in a warm-to-cool gradient, gold on top and sea blue on the bottom. That tells me something already. Sun and ocean are going to matter. Continue is greyed out, so no save. There's a build stamp in the corner, "2026-11-20 save 21", which looks like a dev label left in. New Game.

Mark The opening cards. "In a world covered by endless water, people live on scattered islands, venturing across the ocean at their own peril." Then sea monsters, storms that swallow ships, and "miracles and oddities are daily routine." I like that last phrase. It sets the expectation that strange things are normal here, so the game doesn't have to stop and explain every wonder.

Mark "Far to the northeast lies a small, verdant island, untouched by the troubles of the wider world. Here live a warmhearted young cook and a headstrong, devout priestess..." So two leads, and the trailing dots are doing the work of saying "untouched" won't last. Classic quiet-village opening. Then a place card: "Kulāiwi Village, The last day of Summer." Last day of summer is a loaded date. Something ends tonight.

Mark And we're in. Our lead is Kailo, red bandana, standing in front of three baskets. First line: "Stew for the wake tonight... cakes and breadfruit for the next island." A wake. So someone died. The intro called the island untouched, and the first thing we hear about is a funeral feast. A lore page popped up too, "'Ulu", which I'm guessing is breadfruit. The game is logging vocabulary for me as it goes. Good sign for a world with its own words.

Mark Melia runs in shouting about boars in the taro patch, three of them. Kailo's reaction is the giveaway for the character: "That patch feeds three houses!" He thinks about food as people. Wait, the game hasn't actually said he or she for Kailo yet. I'll hold off.

Mark Hina arrives, flower in her hair, and she's dry. "Then stop shouting and do something about it." And then, "I was coming over for one of your taro cakes. I'd like there to still be taro tomorrow." That's a nice line. She's the priestess, I assume, the headstrong one. Practical and a little sharp, but she came over for cake.

Mark Melia wants to come and fight with a sling. Kailo tells her to stay and guard the stew from gulls. Melia's the kid who wants in. I'd bet money she gets in eventually.

Mark Cut to the taro patch, boars in the turnips, Kailo yelling "Shoo! Shoo!" and the boar answering "SQUEEEE!" It's light. Then a banner, "Preemptive strike!", and Hina: "You do remember how to fight, right?"

Mark Battle screen. Kailo 130 HP, Hina 160. Each has two small element icons next to the name, Kailo has a fire one and a water-ish swirl, Hina a flower and a gold star. Under each HP bar are seven small empty marks. I don't know what those are yet. Hina is asking me a yes or no. I'm a first-time player and I want the lesson, so I'm answering No. A game asking this is usually offering a tutorial without calling it one, the way Chrono Trigger's fair or Earthbound's first fights hand it to you.

Mark "...Really? You lunkhead. Fine, I'll remind you." Ha. So the tutorial is framed as Hina teasing Kailo, which keeps it in character. She explains the column of faces on the right is turn order, top goes now, and that "What you pick decides how soon you go again." That's a timeline system, closer to Grandia or Final Fantasy X than to a plain round. Good, that's a real decision every turn.

Mark Two boars on the field. Melia said three. I'll keep an eye out for the third.

Mark The command list is Attack, Cook, Element, Item. Cook. The cook's personal command is Cook. I like that already.

Mark Attack opens a second panel, and now I recognize it. Y is a 1, X is a 2, A is a 3, with a yellow stamina bar on the side. "Those yellow bars are stamina. Every stroke spends some." This is the Chrono Cross attack chain. Light, Medium, Strong, each one cheaper and more accurate or more expensive and riskier, and the hit chance shown on the button. The Light showed 88%.

Mark Light hit, 5 damage, and the next Light now reads 98%. Then 100%. "Each hit you land makes the next likelier." And the seven little marks under the HP bar are mana, which fills from hits. Then she tells me Strong tires you most and pushes you down the order, but builds more mana. I swing Strong: 41. That's eight times the Light. Mana jumps from two marks to five.

Mark She tells me to show the pig my Sauté. I backed out of the chain and the cursor jumped to Element on its own, which surprised me. Sauté turned out to be listed there with another entry called "Lelua". So Sauté is an element, not something under Cook. I'm not sure yet what the difference between Cook and Element is going to be.

Mark Sauté: 13. Less than my Strong did, and it cost one mana mark. The number popped over the top boar but the fire burst drew on the bottom one. Either the animation is pointing at the wrong boar or it splashed. Only one number came up, so I think it's a drawing mismatch. I'll note it.

Mark Hina's turn. She makes me Defend, "Go on, humor me." Defend is one press left on the command list, and gives a single mana mark. Fine.

Mark Hina's own command is "Scold." Kailo cooks and Hina scolds. That's a real character note built into the menu, and it makes me smile. I chained Light, Light, then a Strong that missed with "Dodge," then backed out and used Scold on the same boar.

Mark Scold opened a mash meter, "Scold!", three seconds. I sat there reading it and lost two of the seconds before I started hitting A. My fault. The boar is now "Sulking", which I'm reading as a status that stops it acting or slows it. But then "Hina is out of breath!" and her mana marks went red, and her face has dropped off the turn order entirely. So there's a cost to tacking a Skill onto the end of a full chain. Nobody told me that. I'll watch what red means.

Mark Kailo's turn again. Light missed at 88%, then two Lights at 6 each, then Strong for 35. That's a lot of damage into this boar and it's still standing. These are farm pests and they're soaking a hundred-plus. Either the numbers are scaled big or this is a sturdier pig than it looks.

Mark Something I noticed by accident. When I hover a command, the turn order on the right redraws as a preview. On Cook, Kailo's face vanished from the whole column. On Element, he stayed in it. So that's what "out of breath" is: using your character's own command after a full chain empties you, and you lose a stretch of turns. Hina did exactly that to herself. The preview is a smart tool. It answers the question before you commit, but nobody pointed at it. I only saw it because I was looking at the column.

Mark Kailo's Element list has two entries. "Lelua" with his two little icons beside it, fire lit, and Sauté under it with a 1, the mana cost. Selecting Lelua offers to switch to "Wai'Kenpō", and the moves below go blank. So Lelua and Wai'Kenpō are his two stances, fire and water, two fighting styles, and switching costs the turn. Wai'Kenpō has nothing in it yet. I'll stay in fire. Sauté again, 12.

Mark Now the boars' turn, and here's the block meter. Three lanes, Y on top, X middle, A bottom. Hina's lesson: "Each bar shows its button." "Press that one as the bar crosses the yellow. Middle hurts least." "Dead center and you parry it. Not that you'll manage." That last line is pure Hina. It's a Mario and Luigi or Paper Mario idea, timed defense, but by lane and button, like a rhythm game. I like that the buttons for defense match the buttons for attack. Light is Y both ways.

Mark First set: the Light I parried perfectly, both Strongs I only caught, 13 and 10 to Kailo. Second set on Hina, same thing. Light perfect, Strongs caught, 20 and 14. I'm consistently a little late on the second blow.

Mark I'm going to look that up, because I want to know what "catch" means against a parry and why my Strong timing is off. Booklet, the parry section. It says a catch anywhere in the zone cuts damage, more the closer to center, and dead center is "Parried!" and does no harm. And this: "every blow that lands on you hurries the ones after it." That's my problem. The first blow lands and the next one speeds up, so a rhythm I planned from the start is late by the end. Also, parry every blow of one attack dead center and you strike back with a strong stroke. That's a real reward. The booklet answered it cleanly. The game itself never told me about the hurrying, and that rule is invisible on the screen.

Mark Kailo finishes boar A with another chain, Strong for 40, "Wild Boar A falls." That boar took about a hundred and seventy. Then Sauté on boar B, and this time it says "Weak!" and does 24, double what boar A took. Same sprite, same element, different answer. I'm filing that away.

Mark Hina's Element list shows her two stances, "Māla Hula" with the flower icon and the gold star second. Under it, Heal, greyed out. Her mana is still red. So red mana is a debt: you can't spend it. I tried attacking, and here's how the debt pays back. Her first Light took her from two red to one red, the second to zero, then the Strong put three blue in. Hits pay off the red first. Once I saw it I liked it. It means "out of breath" isn't just lost turns, it's a hole you have to climb out of with plain attacks.

Mark Her attacks on boar B say "Resisted." 2 damage, 2, then 11. And then she says it herself, "You're bleeding on the taro. Hold still, I'll Heal you." Heal on Kailo, +18.

Mark "Wild Boar B shifts to fire stance." There it is. The monsters have stances too. That's why one boar was weak to fire and the other wasn't, and why Hina's flower-stance hits were resisted. Then the boar uses Sauté, the same element Kailo has, on Hina. "Weak!" 43. Flower is weak to fire. A boar sautéing a priestess. I'll allow it.

Mark So now the puzzle's clear enough to act on. Kailo's fire Light on a fire boar: "Resisted," 3. Plain attacks carry the stance's element. I switch Kailo to Wai'Kenpō, the water stance. "Kailo shifts into Wai'Kenpō." His max HP went from 130 to 160 when he switched. Stances change your stats, not just your element. Then the boar's fire on Kailo: "Resisted," 7. Good trade.

Mark His water hits land for a neutral 5, not weak. So water doesn't simply beat fire here, or not on this boar. I don't know the wheel yet. I'm not going to guess it from one fight.

Mark Hina to her other stance, "Shakan Mudras." Mudras are hand gestures, so I'm guessing the star is a light or sun element, maybe spirit. Her max HP dropped to 130, current scaled down with it. And her Heal is gone from the list. Heal lives in the flower stance only. That's a real trade: the stance that's safe against fire can't heal.

Mark "Wild Boar B digs up a truffle." Two turns later, "Wild Boar B eats the truffle." +30. Hina: "Lanu's beasts always heal. Nature heals, but it burns." First proper name of something above the world. Lanu. A god, or a spirit of nature, and the island's animals are "his" or "hers". "Nature heals, but it burns" is half a battle hint and half a proverb.

Design notes

1. The battle system has real depth from fight one: turn order you can preview, Chrono Cross attack chains, mana from hits, stances that change element, HP and move list, enemy stances that change weaknesses. This is the game's strongest asset. Large, praise.

2. The parry meter is excellent. Lanes match the attack buttons, patterns are readable per monster, and a full perfect set triggers a counter. Parrying seven Aghoy Lights in a row and seeing a 42 counter was the best moment of the hour. Large, praise.

3. The first fight is too long and too dense. Two farm boars took about thirteen minutes, soak roughly 170 HP each, heal with truffles and sleep. Cut their HP or the healing for the tutorial fight. Large.

4. The tutorial covers about half the rules. Out of breath, red mana, enemy stances, cooking being interruptible, and the turn order preview are left to discovery. A careful player can work them out; most players will be confused and blame the game. One extra Hina line for each would fix it. Large.

5. The turn order preview on hover is a superb tool, and nobody points at it. Hina should say "watch the faces move when you pick a command." Medium.

6. Cook interrupt plus ingredient loss is a harsh first lesson. Losing the dish, all mana and the ingredients to one 3-damage hit, with no warning, felt like a trap. Either warn the first time (the Poi icon could appear in the turn order, so the player sees the boar before it) or refund the food. Medium.

7. Strong hit chance should be shown on its button like the Light. Without it the Strong is a blind gamble and Light-Light-Medium-Medium is simply correct. Medium.

8. Mana with nowhere to go. In Wai'Kenpō Kailo sat on 7 full marks for several fights with nothing to spend them on. A full meter that can't be used reads as a broken promise. Make sure each stance has at least one element early. Medium.

9. Stances as story: Hina's two stances are the two sides of the island's schism, Lanu's dance and Haka's mudras, and Kailo's are both imported arts. The menu tells you who these people are. Character commands Cook and Scold do the same. Large, praise.

10. Totems as save points framed as prayer, the Lanu post offering that silently opens a quest, the Loremaster's "Nobody ever told me I had to pick one." The world-building is woven into systems instead of sitting in text dumps. Large, praise.

11. The fetch quest is eighteen items with no hint where most come from until you find the Boy in an inn two screens away. Put that information with Noelani, who gave the list. Medium.

12. Pork drop rate. A quest item from the most common enemy should drop often, or the game should say it's rare. Five boars, no pork. Medium.

13. Hen eggs: the chase is intended ("if you are quick"), but the narrator and the walk both put me on the tile the hen just left. A small "the hen holds still for a beat when you face it" rule would keep the joke and lose the frustration. Small.

14. Visible monsters on the map with ambush and preemptive banners make every fight feel like my choice. Good. Small, praise.

15. Fight length outside the tutorial is right: Papanok and Aghoy fights took two to three minutes. Small, praise.

16. Lore and glossary pages fill as you hear words. Fine idea, but they flash past at the top of the screen during dialogue and it's easy to miss what was added. Small.

17. The Haka and Shaka explanation is good world-building. Mauna/Mauga and Moanaku/Moanatu without that explanation read as typos. Small.

18. Quest entry names Noelani as the source of the Great Boar quest; Melia told it. Small, probably a bug.

19. Money arrives silently. I reached 265 cowries without noticing; the grocer and the inn quoted prices I thought I couldn't pay. Make the gain visible. Small.

20. Writing is consistently good: short, specific, funny without breaking tone, and the grief around Pono and the father is handled lightly. Large, praise.

Story and characters

The story so far is small on purpose: a wake for an old man, a ruined stew, boars that keep coming. Underneath it there's a century-old religious split over who owns the sun, a mana theology that matches the battle system, and a hint that something in the woods is wrong. The tone is warm and dry. I like it more than I expected from a fetch-quest opening.

- Kailo: the cook, the lead. He thinks about food as people ("That patch feeds three houses!"). Quiet when it matters ("...the wake's tonight."). His father is gone, "the loudest man on this beach." Review so far

Nuitai is trying to be a Chrono Cross-style RPG set in a Pacific island world, where cooking, faith and family are as important as fighting. After an hour I think the fighting is the best thing in it. The attack chain, the stance system and above all the lane-based parry meter give every fight something to read and something to execute, and the counter-strike for a perfect parry is a great reward. The world is written with care: the totems, the Lanu posts, the two religions living side by side, a cook who fights with a frying pan and a priestess whose move list is a theological argument. The dialogue is short and lands.

What doesn't work yet is the on-ramp. The first fight is long, the tutorial explains half the rules, and several of the rules it skips will punish a new player hard, especially the ruined dish. Some meters lie to you (full mana with nothing to spend, no hit chance on Strong). The opening quest is eighteen ingredients with the key information hidden two screens away, and the most obvious ingredient never dropped.

Would I recommend it today? To someone who likes Chrono Cross and enjoys figuring out systems, yes, with a warning that the first twenty minutes are rough. To a general RPG player, not yet. Fix the tutorial fight's length, add the five missing explanations, and show the numbers the player needs, and I'd recommend it to most people.
