Food

What Nuitai eats, by the people who make it: where each dish is most at home, and whose favorite it is.

The islandsThe BandariThe KameFrom the potNot yet placed

The islands39

Akai lau
Green onion, a slim plant with long hollow leaves and a white base. It grows quickly from a seed, and its leaves and bulbs flavor stews and soups.Found in: everywhere
'Awa
A drink of the pounded root steeped in water, bitter and calming, shared at ceremonies and gatherings.Found in: everywhere
'Awapuhi
Wild ginger, a plant with a pale root that grows in the earth. The root is sharp and sweet, and goes into cooking and into medicine.Found in: everywhere
Blood guava
A red-fleshed fruit that grows best under cloudy skies.Found in: Matua · Favorite of Kiki
Breadfruit
A large green fruit from a tall tree, called 'ulu. Cooked, it is soft and starchy like bread or potato. One tree feeds a family for years.Found in: everywhere
Coconut
The fruit of the coconut palm, called niu. Its sweet water is drunk fresh, and its white flesh is eaten or grated into puddings. Palms line nearly every beach.Found in: everywhere
Eel
A long, slippery fish and the rarest catch of the fishing grounds. It is grilled or smoked.Found in: Matua · Favorite of Tawiri
Egg
A bird's egg, boiled, fried or kneaded into noodles.Found in: everywhere
Fried egg
An egg fried in a little salt.Found in: everywhere
Haupia
A firm, sweet coconut pudding set with arrowroot and cut in squares.Found in: Māla Iki · Favorite of Maika
'Inamona
A relish of roasted kukui nuts ground with salt. It is stirred into fish and seaweed dishes.Found in: everywhere
Kohuapua
A braise of pork and eggs in coconut, with fish, ginger, shoyu, sweet potato and green onion, cooked slowly until the sauce goes dark and sweet. It is served at wakes and feasts. Every family's is a little different, and each swears theirs is the right one.Found in: Māla Iki
Kukui nut
A hard oily nut, also called the candlenut. It is roasted and ground, or pressed for its oil.Found in: everywhere
Kulolo
A dense, sweet pudding of grated taro and coconut, baked slowly until it turns dark and chewy. It is served at feasts and given as a gift.Found in: Māla Iki
Laulau
Pork wrapped in ti leaves and steamed in a bundle until the meat is tender. The leaf is not eaten; it gives the meat its smell.Found in: Māla Iki
Mackerel
A small, oily, silver fish, plentiful near shore.Found in: everywhere
Mahi-mahi
A bright green and gold fish of the open water, firm and mild.Found in: everywhere
Mullet
A fish that feeds in shallow bays and river mouths.Found in: everywhere
Noni
A lumpy pale fruit with a strong smell, pressed for its juice or baked soft. It is more medicine than meal.Found in: everywhere
Noni juice
Noni pressed with water into a sour drink.Found in: everywhere
Palusami
Taro leaves wrapped around coconut cream and baked in the earth oven.Found in: Velafi · Favorite of Olola
Pia
Arrowroot, a starchy root grated and washed to a fine white flour. It sets coconut milk into a pudding.Found in: everywhere
Pineapple
A spiked fruit with sweet, sharp yellow flesh, grown on low bushes.Found in: everywhere
Poi
Taro root pounded with water into a smooth paste, eaten fresh or left a day or two to turn sour.Found in: Māla Iki
Pork
Meat from the wild boars of the islands. It has to be cooked through; raw, it makes most people sick.Found in: everywhere
Pua'a Ananá
A pork roast cooked with pieces of pineapple, the sweet and the salt together.Found in: everywhere
Roast fowl
A bird roasted whole over the fire.Found in: everywhere · Favorite of Leilagi
Roast pig
A whole pig cooked in the earth oven for a feast.Found in: everywhere · Favorite of Paku
Salt
Gathered from the sea. It seasons nearly everything and cures what must keep.Found in: everywhere
Seaweed
Sea plants gathered on the beach and eaten fresh, toasted, or simmered into broth. Pressed in thin sheets, it is nori.Found in: everywhere
Shoyu
A dark salty sauce brewed from soybeans and salt. The bottle that matters most in Kulāiwi came from Māla Iki.Found in: Māla Iki
Smoked eel
Eel salted and smoked over a slow fire until it keeps.Found in: Matua · Favorite of Tawiri
Snapper
A red or pink reef fish with firm white flesh.Found in: everywhere
Taro
A starchy root grown in flooded patches; its leaves are eaten too.Found in: everywhere
Ti leaf
A long glossy leaf used to wrap food for steaming. Nobody eats it.Found in: everywhere
Tuna
A heavy, fast fish of deep water, with dark red flesh.Found in: everywhere
'Uala
The sweet potato, a sweet, starchy root baked, steamed or mashed. Island voyagers carried it across the sea long ago, and it grows well in poor soil.Found in: everywhere
'Ulu poi
A paste of mashed breadfruit and water, made like poi from taro. It is a little sweeter and a little thicker.Found in: everywhere
Wheat
A grain grown in patches, kneaded into noodles.Found in: everywhere

The Bandari10

Candied crickets
Crickets fried crisp and coated in sugar, sold by the scoop.Found in: Kota Emas · Favorite of Asap Setia
Coconut rice
Rice steamed in coconut milk, the everyday staple.Found in: Matahari · Favorite of Sarjana
Coffee
Brought by the Bandari, grown on Matahari's volcanic slopes and sold across the ring.Found in: Matahari
Gula kopi
Hard coffee candies made from Matahari coffee.Found in: Matahari · Favorite of Kailo, as a snack
Kinilaw
Raw fish cured in vinegar and lime, eaten cold.Found in: Bayang Seas · Favorite of Ren
Pandan cake
A soft green cake flavored with pandan, a screw-pine leaf that tastes sweet and grassy, like vanilla. Offered at shrines.Found in: Matahari · Favorite of Hina
Rice
The grain of the Bandari, boiled or steamed; coconut rice is the everyday staple.Found in: Matahari
Satay
Meat on skewers grilled over coals, with a spicy sauce.Found in: Matahari · Favorite of Damar (goat)
Soto
A hot, spiced, yellow soup, often with chicken.Found in: Matahari · Favorite of Karvara
Sugarcane
Cane stalks chewed raw for the sweet juice.Found in: Matahari

The Kame2

Saimin
Noodles in a light broth with an egg or a slice of fish cake on top; a Kame dish that spread east over centuries of contact and is popular in Lanuloa.Found in: Kame Kingdom · Favorite of Kailo, his signature dish
Sea grapes (umibudō, umibuduu)
A seaweed of tiny green beads that pop in the mouth, eaten raw.Found in: Kame Kingdom · Favorite of Gen'emon, with sake
