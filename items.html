Item glossary

Things a player can find, wear or carry in Nuitai, and what each one is. Most come from the real islands of the Pacific and Southeast Asia, and every name here is the real word for now, until the game gives it its own.

WornJewelry and charmsWeaponsShieldsCarried and usedNot in a bag, but named

Worn13

Putong
A head cloth wound like a turban or tied like a bandana. A red one marks someone who has fought in battle; an embroidered one, someone who has fought many. Jecht and Wakka wear something close.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Pre-colonial Philippines
Bahag
A loincloth: a long strip of cloth wrapped between the legs and round the waist. Everyday wear for a fighter.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Tapis
A wrapped skirt, a cloth tucked at the waist.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Malong
A tube of patterned cloth that is a skirt, a cloak, a sling, a hammock or a blanket, worn a dozen ways.Found in: Lanuloa, Bayang · Real source: Mindanao
Baro
A short collarless shirt or jacket, often worn open.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Salakot
A wide cone-shaped hat, woven, sometimes capped in silver or tortoiseshell.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Sarong
A wrapped cloth, the everyday garment of the west.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Malay, Indonesian
Songket
Cloth woven with gold thread. A treasure as much as a garment.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Malay, Indonesian
Batik
Cloth dyed in patterns with wax.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Indonesian
Ikat
Cloth whose threads are dyed before weaving, so the pattern blurs at the edges.Found in: Bandari, Lanuloa · Real source: Island Southeast Asia
Kebaya
A fitted blouse.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Malay, Indonesian
Tengkolok
A folded head cloth, stiff and shaped, for rank and ceremony.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Malay
Hand wraps
Cloth wound round the hands and wrists, for fighting and for grip. Worn by nearly every fighter.Found in: Everywhere · Real source: Gubat Banwa art

Jewelry and charms4

Lingling-o
An ear ornament shaped like the letter omega, in jade or gold. Worn across the Pacific's island peoples for two thousand years.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines, Taiwan, Vietnam
Kamagi
A heavy braided gold chain.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Anting-anting
An amulet: a medallion, a stone, a written charm, carried for protection or luck. Could be an accessory that gives a status protection.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Shell money
Strings of small shell discs, the Fire Islands' money.Found in: Fire Islands · Real source: Solomon Islands

Weapons59

Pedang
Malay and Indonesian for a sword; the straight sword of silat. Damar's first weapon.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Malay, Indonesian
Kampilan
A long single-edged war sword, wide toward a flared, almost squared tip, with a forked hilt. The squarish sword the author liked. Ren's first weapon.Found in: Lanuloa, Bayang · Real source: Mindanao and the Visayas
Kalis
A sword with a wavy blade.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Barong
A leaf-shaped blade, heavy at the front.Found in: Bayang · Real source: Sulu
Bolo
An everyday chopping knife, for brush, coconuts and fights.Found in: Lanuloa · Real source: Philippines
Parang
A heavy chopping blade, the bolo's western cousin.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Malay, Indonesian
Mandau
A sword with a carved bone or horn hilt and hair tassels. Karvara's first weapon.Found in: Bandari · Real source: Dayak of Borneo
Katana
The curved Japanese sword. Only for a sword user from the northwest.Found in: the northwest · Real source: Japan
Frying pan
Kailo's first weapon.Found in: Māla Iki
Shovel
Sarjana's first weapon, from his digs.Found in: Kota Emas
Mortar and pestle
Kiki's first weapon, for grinding medicine.Found in: Matua
Uchiwa
A flat paper fan cooks use to fan a charcoal grill. Cooking things are tools.Real source: Japan
Khakkhara
A Buddhist monk's ringed staff; the rings jingle as he walks. Shakujō in Japanese. Tahji's.Found in: Kota Emas · Real source: Buddhist Asia
Tokotoko
A carved Māori walking stick, the kind a speaker holds on the marae. Tawiri's first weapon, hung with feathers.Found in: Matua · Real source: Māori

Hawaiian for a staff or a cane. Maika's first weapon.Found in: Māla Iki · Real source: Hawaii
Gohei
The Shinto paper wand. Hina's first weapon.Real source: Japan

A gohei from the temple of Kota Emas. Tahji's first weapon; the name is invented.Found in: Kota Emas
